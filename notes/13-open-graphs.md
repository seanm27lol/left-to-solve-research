# Tree embeddings, coloring, and path covers

## Sumner's universal tournament conjecture

ID `SumnerUniversalTournamentConjecture.sumner_conjecture`, kind `open`. [Left to Solve](https://lefttosolve.com/p/SumnerUniversalTournamentConjecture.sumner_conjecture). The question is whether every tournament of order 2n−2 contains every oriented n-vertex tree, n≥2, as a subgraph. Kühn, Mycroft and Osthus proved this for all sufficiently large n. The all-orders bound is stronger. A 2026 preprint of Ai and Lian reports the uniform bound ceil((18n−23)/7); see its separate treatment of bi-arborescences. [Large-order theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4430) · [2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11667).

**Exact result for every orientation of every star.** Write a for the number of leaves directed out of the center, b for the number directed in, and k=a+b=n−1. When a,b>0, the smallest order forcing this star in every tournament is 2k−1. For a pure in-star or out-star the answer is 2k. These are independent elementary calculations, with no priority claim.

For mixed orientations let L contain vertices of outdegree at most a−1. The tournament induced by L has average outdegree (|L|−1)/2, so |L|≤2a−1. Similarly at most 2b−1 vertices have indegree at most b−1. Their union has at most 2k−2 vertices. Any outside vertex supplies the star because its in-neighbors and out-neighbors are disjoint. At order 2k−2 take a regular tournament on 2a−1 vertices and one on 2b−1 vertices, with all edges directed from the second block into the first. Every vertex in the first block has outdegree a−1 and every vertex in the second has indegree b−1. Regular tournaments of odd order 2h+1 exist by directing from i to i+1 through i+h cyclically. For a pure out-star, average outdegree (2k−1)/2 at order 2k forces a vertex of outdegree at least k, and the regular tournament on 2k−1 lacks one. Reverse edges for the in-star.

**Route to the next branching case.** Fix candidate images x→y of the two centers of an oriented double star. Each of its four leaf types has a neighbor set among the other vertices: out- or in-neighbors of x, and out- or in-neighbors of y. Hall's theorem gives an exact extension criterion: for each of the 15 nonempty sets of types, the union of their candidate sets must have size at least the sum of their prescribed leaf counts. Testing four vertex degrees alone misses overlaps between neighborhoods at opposite centers. The unsolved step for this approach is forcing a pair satisfying all these inequalities simultaneously and then controlling incompatibilities for an arbitrary center tree.

## The Gyárfás–Sumner conjecture

ID `GyarfasSumnerConjecture.gyarfas_sumner_conjecture`, kind `open`. [Left to Solve](https://lefttosolve.com/p/GyarfasSumnerConjecture.gyarfas_sumner_conjecture). For every fixed tree T, does excluding T as an induced subgraph bound chromatic number in terms of clique number? Nguyen, Scott and Seymour prove that bounded clique number and sufficiently large chromatic number force a rooted copy in which every path from the root is induced. Edges between different branches remain the obstruction. [Graphs and Combinatorics (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-024-02754-z).

**All stars.** For T=K_(1,s), s≥2, write w=ω(G). A neighborhood cannot contain an s-element stable set or a w-clique. Hence each degree is strictly smaller than the Ramsey number R(s,w), and greedy coloring gives χ(G)≤R(s,w). This argument bounds maximum degree, which the general tree-free condition need not do.

**All paths.** For a fixed t≥3 put f(1)=1 and f(w)=1+(t−2)f(w−1). Every induced-P_t-free graph of clique number at most w has chromatic number at most f(w). This is an elementary version of the classical induced-path argument. Suppose a connected component H has larger chromatic number. Every neighborhood in H has chromatic number at most a=f(w−1) by induction on w. Start at any vertex v1 and choose in H−v1 a component C1 of maximum chromatic number. Then χ(C1)≥χ(H)−1 and v1 has a neighbor in C1. Maintain an induced path v1,...,vi and a connected set Ci touched by vi and anticomplete to earlier path vertices. In Ci remove N(vi), costing at most a colors. A remaining component of maximum chromatic number has a neighbor vi+1 in Ci∩N(vi), by connectivity. Keep that component as Ci+1. Each extension costs at most a colors, so after t−2 extensions C_(t−1) still has positive chromatic number. Append a neighbor of v_(t−1) in it. This gives the forbidden induced path. The gap for a branching T is that this step preserves only one component of large chromatic number; two subsequent branches can also be joined by unwanted edges.

**All tree shapes in high-girth hosts.** If |T|=t≥2 and G has girth greater than t and χ(G)≥t, take a t-or-higher chromatic vertex-critical induced subgraph, of minimum degree at least t−1. Embed T from a root, adding one child at a time. There are at most t−2 already used alternatives around its parent's image, so a free neighbor exists. Any extra edge among the images closes a cycle of length at most t. Thus the copy is induced. This loses control as soon as short cycles may connect different branches. Branch interaction, rather than greedily finding enough vertices, is the exact extra issue identified by the path-induced theorem above.

## Written on the Wall II, Conjecture 372

ID `WrittenOnTheWallII.GraphConjecture372.conjecture372`, kind `open`. [Left to Solve](https://lefttosolve.com/p/WrittenOnTheWallII.GraphConjecture372.conjecture372). For a tree T of order greater than two and vertex-disjoint path cover number p≥2 (singleton paths allowed), the conjecture states τ ≤ γ_t(p−1), where τ is the minimum vertex cover size and γ_t the minimum total dominating set size. The upstream file cites Gebendorfer's treatment of trees covered by two paths. Public proof reports also document the stronger additive inequality below. The argument here was obtained independently; the theorem has prior work. [Upstream declaration and references](https://google-deepmind.github.io/formal-conjectures/src/FormalConjectures/WrittenOnTheWallII/GraphConjecture372/) · [Gebendorfer](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.25035.55849) · [Additive-bound verification report](https://aiproofwatch.com/problems/tree-cover-domination-additive-bound).

**Theorem.** If p≥2, then τ≤γ_t+p−2. For p=1, τ≤γ_t. Consequently all three catalog formulations hold, counting this as one mathematical problem.

**Proof.** Let n be the order and D a minimum total dominating set; write d=|D|. A minimum path cover consists of p disjoint paths whose contraction is a tree, with p−1 edges between paths. Every vertex is adjacent to D. Summing degrees over D, contributors internal to their own path give at most two each, and the edges between paths give at most two each. By König's theorem τ is the maximum size of a matching, hence 2τ≤n. Thus

2τ ≤ n ≤ sum over v∈D of deg(v) ≤ 2d+2(p−1).

When p=1 this suffices. For p≥2 it remains to exclude equality at both ends. Suppose equality holds. The tree has a perfect matching. Every vertex has exactly one neighbor in D. Every vertex of D is internal to its own path. Both endpoints of every edge between paths belong to D.

Choose a leaf path P of the contracted tree, and call its unique attachment vertex u. It belongs to D and has its unique D-neighbor across the attachment edge. All other vertices of P have exactly one D-neighbor on P. The neighborhoods within P of the vertices of D∩P therefore partition P−{u} into pairs, each consisting of the two positions on either side of its center. Thus P has odd order. Its perfect matching in T must use the attachment edge; the two segments of P−{u} must both have even order. Number the positions 1,...,m. It follows that u is at an odd position.

The odd positions to the left of u are partitioned into consecutive pairs of odd positions by these same neighborhoods: a pair cannot jump over the missing odd position u. There are consequently an even number of odd positions to its left, so u≡1 mod 4. All even positions of P must also be partitioned into consecutive pairs. The pairing is forced successively from the left: (2,4),(6,8), and so forth. Every odd center in D∩P must therefore occur at an index ≡3 mod 4. This contradicts u∈D. Equality is impossible and integrality gives τ≤d+p−2. Finally d+p−2≤d(p−1) because (d−1)(p−2)≥0. □

**Finite audit.** Independently enumerating all 435 non-isomorphic trees of orders 2 through 11, all vertex subsets for domination and vertex cover, and all edge-deletion sets for path cover confirmed the inequality. Eighteen trees with p≥2 attained the additive bound in this range. Reproducible checker: `the accompanying verification scripts`.
