# Divisor averages, Stirling sums, and integer sequences

## A346644 — Every positive integer is the reduced denominator of an average of divisors

Write the target as n = 2^a m, m odd, and set d = 2^a, B = 2^d − 1, L = m B. Choose an odd prime p ≡ −1 (mod 2m), which exists by Dirichlet's theorem, including when m=1. Set k = 2^(d−1) p^(L−1). Multiplicativity on coprime factors gives σ(k)/τ(k) = B(1+p+⋯+p^(L−1))/(dL) = (1+p+⋯+p^(L−1))/n. The numerator is odd since L is odd. Modulo m its L terms alternate 1,−1 and sum to 1, so its gcd with n is 1. This proves existence, hence the least k exists by well-ordering. It covers d=1, L=1 and n=1. A single power of two is insufficient: n=6 would give σ(2^5)/τ(2^5)=63/6=21/2. The second prime factor is what absorbs cancellation. Benoit Cloitre's entry dates to 2021. [Conjecture](https://oeis.org/A346644) · [companion denominator sequence](https://oeis.org/A057021) · [Dirichlet, MIT lecture 18, Theorem 18.1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_full_lec.pdf).

## A354478 — The reciprocal signed Stirling sum tends to 1

Let c(n,k)=|s(n,k)| and S(n)=Σ_{k=1}^n 1/s(n,k). Then c(n,n)=1 and c(n,n−1)=n(n−1)/2. For n≥4 and 1≤k<n, c(n,k)≥n(n−1)/2. The base row for n=4 is (6,11,6,1). For k≤n−2, the recurrence c(n,k)=(n−1)c(n−1,k)+c(n−1,k−1) and induction give at least (n−1) times binom(n−1,2), which is ≥binom(n,2) for n≥5. The remaining k=n−1 was given explicitly. Thus |S(n)−1| ≤ (n−1)/binom(n,2)=2/n for n≥4. The signed convention matters in the definition (S(2)=0), but the absolute tail estimate handles the signs. A second proof of the lower bound is log-concavity of the unsigned row (real-rooted rising factorial) and the endpoint values (n−1)! and binom(n,2). [Conjecture, V. Kotesovec](https://oeis.org/A354478) · [denominators](https://oeis.org/A354479) · [NIST DLMF §26.8, definitions and recurrence](https://dlmf.nist.gov/26.8).

## A372239 — Both congruences for prime-indexed coefficients

For a(n)=[x^n](1+2x)/((1−2x)√(1−4x)), let p be an odd prime and h=(p−1)/2. Write C_j=binom(2j,j). Expanding the prefactor gives a(p)=C_p+2^(p+1) Σ_{j=0}^{p−1} 2^(−j) C_j. In the field F_p, C_p=2 by the identity (1+x)^(2p)=(1+x^p)^2. For 0≤j<p, C_j=(−4)^j binom(−1/2,j)≡(−4)^j binom(h,j) since j! is invertible. In particular the j>h terms vanish. The sum is (1−2)^h=(−1)^h. Fermat gives 2^(p+1)≡4, so a(p)≡2+4(−1)^h, i.e. 6 for p≡1 (mod 4) and −2 for p≡3 (mod 4). Checks include p=3, a(3)=76≡1=−2, and p=5, a(5)=916≡1=6. A separate derivation factors (1−4x)^(−1/2) as (1−4x)^h (1−4x^p)^(−1/2) modulo p before coefficient extraction. [Mélika Tebni's defining entry and coefficient formula](https://oeis.org/A372239).

## A371918 — Greedy exponents of powers of two form a permutation

For n=0,1,… choose the least previously unused positive exponent e such that the decimal digits of 2^e contain the decimal string for n. Step n=0 exists: 2^10=1024. For n≥1 put d=1+floor(log10 n). The interval (log10(n)−d+1, log10(n+1)−d+1) is a nonempty subinterval of [0,1]. The fractional parts of e log10(2) visit it infinitely often: log10(2) is irrational by unique factorization and every tail of an irrational rotation is dense. At all sufficiently large such e, 2^e begins with the digits of n. Only finitely many exponents have already been used, guaranteeing existence at every step. Injectivity is built in. For surjectivity, consider step n=2^e for any positive e. If e is still unused then it qualifies, and no j<e can qualify: a full decimal substring representing the positive integer 2^e cannot appear in a smaller positive integer 2^j. Thus e is used by step 2^e. This last inequality is a uniform deadline, not a density heuristic. [Scott Shannon's original conjecture, including offset zero](https://oeis.org/A371918) · [background on leading digits of powers of two](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/45771?nt=1).

## A379181 · A one-paragraph independent disproof

For each n, any counted word has an integer average digit; thus its digit sum is ≤4n or ≥5n. The digits 0,…,9 have average 4.5. Let r=9/10 and c=(1+r+⋯+r^9)/r^4 <10 (equivalently (1−r^10)/(1−r)<10r^4). Among all length-n decimal strings including leading zeros, the number with digit sum ≤4n is ≤r^(−4n)(1+r+⋯+r^9)^n=c^n, since each such string contributes at least r^(4n) in the weighted sum. Complementing each digit j→9−j bounds the upper tail by the same quantity. Therefore a(n)≤2c^n and limsup a(n)^(1/n)≤c<10. All a(n)>0 (the all-ones string). If a(n+1)/a(n)→10, taking logs and Cesàro means would force a(n)^(1/n)→10, contradiction. The upper bound survives the leading-zero relaxation and all conventions on ties that still require the mean to be an actual digit. A second route via Hoeffding gives a(n)≤2·10^n exp(−n/162). The first proof uses just a geometric series. We do not assert the actual value/existence of a ratio limit. [Spezia's conjecture and exact counting conventions](https://oeis.org/A379181).

## A301311 — Last digit zero and a finite formula

Let n≥0 and P_n(k)=binom(k²+n−1,n), with P_0=1. For each fixed n this is an even integer-valued polynomial of degree 2n (degree 0 for n=0) and for n>0 it vanishes at k=0. The catalog coefficient is L(P_n), where L(P)=(1/3)Σ_{k≥0}(2/3)^kP(k). For a degree-D integer-valued polynomial put c_j=Δ^jP(0) and expand P(k)=Σ_{j=0}^D c_j binom(k,j). Each c_j is an integer and L(binom(k,j))=2^j by the geometric series. Consequently a(n)=Σ_{j=0}^{2n}2^j Σ_{t=0}^j (−1)^(j−t)binom(j,t)binom(t²+n−1,n). In particular a(n)≡c_0=P_n(0) modulo 2. For the mod-5 argument, apply L coefficientwise to (1+z)^(−k). The result is (1+z)/(1+3z) ≡ (1+z)/(1−2z) modulo 5. For j>0 its z^j coefficient is 3·2^(j−1)≡−2^j, while the constant coefficient is 1. Thus L(P(−k))≡2c_0−L(P(k)) (mod 5). Evenness of P_n implies L(P_n)≡c_0 (mod 5). Since c_0=0 for n>0, 10 divides a(n). All weighted sums of polynomial coefficients converge over the reals before reduction; no illicit reduction of an infinite geometric sum modulo 5 is used. Both the finite-expression question and divisibility conjecture in the OEIS source are answered. [Original entry and coefficient formula](https://oeis.org/A301311).

## A071052 — Even-time zero count in Rule 150

Let P_n(x)=(1+x+x²)^n over F_2. Starting from a single on-cell, the rule evolves rows by multiplication by 1+x+x², and the n-th row has exactly 2n+1 positions. In characteristic two, P_{2n}(x)=P_n(x²). Hence the coefficients at even positions copy the n-th row, while all 2n odd positions in the length-(4n+1) row vanish. This proves a(2n)=a(n)+2n for all n≥0. Independent check: Sloane's run-length-transform theorem for ON cells implies its count is invariant under appending a binary zero to n, giving the same result by complementation. This is an existing-literature corollary even though the OEIS entry still labels the formula conjectural. [Conjecture](https://oeis.org/A071052) · [polynomial encoding](https://oeis.org/A071036) · [Sloane 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01168).

## A135052 — Exact five-lag recurrence for lattice-path coefficients

Let F=(1−2x−√R)/(2x²(1−x)) with R=1−4x+8x³−4x⁴, choosing the branch with constant term 1. Let U=(1,−5,4,8,−12,4) and V=(2,−4,2,−20,36,−16). The conjecture is Σ_{j=0}^5 (U_j n+V_j)a(n−j)=0 for n≥5. Put B=ΣU_j x^j=(x−1)(2x²−1)(2x²−4x+1), D=Σ(V_j+j U_j)x^j=4x⁵−12x⁴+4x³+10x²−9x+2. Substituting F and differentiating √R by (√R)'=R'/(2√R) gives xB F'+D F=2−6x+8x²−4x³. This rational-polynomial identity was also simplified exactly with symbolic algebra. Taking any x^n coefficient for n≥5 proves all six coefficients of the claimed recurrence (in fact n≥4 if a(−1)=0). ## A335407 — Exact classification of the zero counts for prime-index anti-runs

The source's proposed list {4,8} is already disproved there by David A. Corneth's n=16. The full zero set is **{4,8,16}**. For a multiset with largest multiplicity b and s remaining items, a permutation without equal neighbors exists iff b≤s+1. Necessity is separation. For sufficiency designate a most frequent symbol X, make b bins, and distribute the other symbols cyclically among the bins in equal-symbol batches. Each batch has length at most b, so each bin contains distinct symbols. The first b−1 bins are nonempty since s≥b−1. The concatenation X B1 X B2 … X Bb has no equal neighbors. Treat the empty multiset as having one empty permutation.

For n!, b=v₂(n!) is maximal by Legendre's formula, and s counts all odd prime factors with multiplicity. For S={3,9,5,25,7,49,11,13,17,19,23,29}, s≥Σ_{h∈S}⌊n/h⌋>nΣ_{h∈S}1/h−12. The rational sum is 409274717998/339658894575>1+12/59. Consequently s>n≥b for n≥59. For the remaining n, starting at d(1)=0, compute d(n)=b−s as d(n−1)+v₂(n)−Ω(n/2^v₂(n)). Exact minimum/maximum validation can be done from this list of d(n) grouped by tens:
- n=1,…,10: 0,1,0,2,1,1,0,3,1,1.
- n=11,…,20: 0,1,0,0,−2,2,1,0,−1,0.
- n=21,…,30: −2,−2,−3,−1,−3,−3,−6,−5,−6,−7.
- n=31,…,40: −8,−3,−5,−5,−7,−7,−8,−8,−10,−8.
- n=41,…,50: −9,−10,−11,−10,−13,−13,−14,−11,−13,−14.
- n=51,…,58: −16,−15,−16,−18,−20,−18,−20,−20.
Thus precisely the three stated n give d(n)>1. The finite audit is exhaustive because the tail inequality begins at an explicit index. [Original statement and n=16 counterexample](https://oeis.org/A335407).
