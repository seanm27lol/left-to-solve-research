# Tilings, binary polynomials, and congruences

## OEIS A401673 — the last two entries of the domicule-tiling triangle

On a 2n by 2n board, match cells that share a side or corner; crossing diagonal matches are permitted. T(n,k) counts matchings with exactly 2k diagonal pieces. Dawid Ryłko (November 2026) conjectured T(n,n²)=1 and T(n,n²−1)=n²(n+1). The first identity includes n=0; the second has n≥1.

Every tile other than a vertical tile crosses between adjacent columns. If no vertical tiles occur, every cell of column 1 must be matched across to column 2, consuming it. Repeat to split the matching into n independent two-column strips. A strip matching gives a permutation of the 2n rows with displacement at most one. Such permutations consist exactly of fixed points and disjoint adjacent transpositions: the first unaccounted row i either maps to i, or maps to i+1, forcing i+1 to map to i by surjectivity. A fixed point gives one horizontal tile; a transposition gives two (crossing) diagonal tiles.

With all tiles diagonal, every strip uses only transpositions and has a unique perfect matching. With exactly two side tiles, they cannot be one horizontal and one vertical: all but the horizontal consume one odd-row cell and one even-row cell, while that horizontal would consume two cells of the same row parity. If both are horizontal, both lie in one strip since the number of fixed points of each 2n-row permutation of this type is even. Choose the strip (n ways) and the positions of its two singletons among the n+1 blocks comprising two singletons and n−1 transpositions. The other strips are forced. Rotation counts the same number with both vertical, giving 2n binom(n+1,2)=n²(n+1). For n=1 the answer is 2, the two ordinary domino tilings. Independent checks: original transfer-matrix rows to n=10; extension to 2r by 2c gives c binom(r+1,2)+r binom(c+1,2).

Sources: https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a401673 and https://oeis.org/A401673/internal . The original credits exact-integer and modular transfer checks and still describes the formulas as conjectures. Related whole-board enumeration: https://github.com/dawidrylko/lattice-dimers ; the top-coefficient proof above needs no transfer enumeration. 

## OEIS A037097 and A037096 — two separate catalog targets with a common proof

For n≥3 let L=2^(n−2), b_k=floor(3^k/2^n) mod 2. In F₂[X], A037097 encodes the polynomial H for the first 2L bits; A037096 encodes P for the first 4L. The original claims are divisibility by (1+X)^(L−1) and (1+X)^(3L−1), respectively. We get exact multiplicities in both.

Induction gives 3^L≡1+2^n modulo 2^(n+1). Start with 3²=9 for n=3. Squaring the congruence doubles the exponent and replaces n by n+1, since the square of the perturbation is 0 modulo 2^(n+2). Multiplying by odd 3^k then gives b_{k+L}=1+b_k in F₂. Put B=sum_{k<L} b_k X^k. Using 1+X^L=(1+X)^L and sum_{k<L}X^k=(1+X)^(L−1), we obtain H=(1+X)^(L−1)[(1+X)B+X^L]. The bracket at X=1 equals one. The second half of the 4L bits repeats H, so P=(1+X^(2L))H=(1+X)^(2L)H. Thus the respective multiplicities are L−1 and 3L−1. The restriction n≥3 matters: for n=2 the bit polynomial vanishes. Independent polynomial division of the bit vectors directly constructed from modular powers checked both exact valuations for n=3,...,11. A different route obtains the same half-word identity from the standard 2-adic valuation of 3^(2^j)−1, and multiplying the half polynomial by its repetition factor gives the full problem already recorded as a relation in OEIS.

Sources: https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a037097 ; https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a037096 ; https://oeis.org/A037097 (includes a July 2026 correction to the exponent), https://oeis.org/A037096 ; https://oeis.org/A055010 gives 3·2^(j−1)−1. Antti Karttunen's entries are from 1999. 

## OEIS A036284 — Fibonacci bit-polynomial divisibility

Write F_0=0, F_1=1. For n≥1, let P=sum_{k<3·2^n} (floor(F_k/2^n) mod 2) X^k in F₂[X]. The source asserts divisibility by (1+X³)^(2^(n−1)−1) and one additional factor 1+X. Put L=2^(n−1), G=1+X³, Q=[[0,1],[1,1]]. For n≥2, Q^(3L)≡(1+2^n)I modulo 2^(n+1). The base is Q^6=[[5,8],[8,13]]≡5I (mod 8). Induct by squaring, including a multiple of 2^(n+1) in the starting matrix: all its contributions to the square are divisible by 2^(n+2). Thus the nth bit of F_(k+3L) differs from that of F_k by F_k mod 2, which repeats 0,1,1. Let B encode the first 3L bits. Combining the two halves gives P=G^(L−1)[G B+X^(3L)(X+X²)]. Indeed the polynomial of the length-3L repeated difference pattern is (X+X²)sum_{j<L}X^(3j)=(X+X²)G^(L−1). The bracket vanishes at X=1; modulo X²+X+1 it equals 1. Hence the proposed full factor divides, and X²+X+1 occurs to exactly exponent L−1. At n=1 the explicit polynomial X³+X⁴ verifies the claim. Do not read the extra (X+1) as an upper bound on multiplicity: at n=2 the quotient by G is X^5(1+X)^2. Direct polynomial division of modular Fibonacci bit vectors checked n=1,...,11.

Sources: https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a036284 , https://oeis.org/A036284 and https://oeis.org/A136380/internal . Classical broader context is D. D. Wall, Fibonacci Series Modulo m (1960), https://doi.org/10.2307/2309169 ; Wall is cited for modular periods, not as a proof of these particular factors. 

## Other independent derivations with known precedents

* A380056. For the exponential coefficients of (exp(x)−1)/cos(2x), multiply by cos(2x). All its positive-degree exponential coefficients vanish mod 4, so a(n)=1 mod 4 for positive n. In characteristic 5 use the binomial convolution product on sequences of exponential coefficients, with E_u=(u^n) and E_u E_v=E_{u+v} (at n=0 use 0^0=1). The cosine is (E_1+E_4)/2 because (2i)²=1 in F5. Multiplying it by 2(E_2−E_4) gives E_1−E_0. The cosine is invertible (constant term 1), so a(n)=2(2^n−4^n) mod 5 in every degree. This proves the divisibility for all positive multiples of four. Checked n=1,...,129. https://oeis.org/A380056 and https://www.trureturing.org/research/oeis-a380056-fourth-index-divisible-by-five/ .
* A392208. Write Y=A', with Y=exp(A Y³). Differentiating yields Y'(1−3 A Y³)=Y^5 and shows recursively all exponential coefficients are integral. In the characteristic-two binomial convolution ring every series with zero constant term has square zero: coefficients with unequal indices cancel in pairs and the positive middle central binomial coefficient is even. Thus Y²=1 and A²=0, so Y'=Y/(1+AY)=Y+A. The initial values (0,1) force the Fibonacci-parity sequence 0,1,1,0,1,1,... . https://oeis.org/A392208 and https://www.trureturing.org/knowledge/node/7dcc8961f9968279d37754e9cc8bb173adccd26d6e4bf12b38229a813c59bdc7/ .
* A393171, A393172, A389536. Let C=x+C², so modulo 2 C=sum_{j>=0}x^(2^j). For A393171 let F=x+F(C)^2. This fixed point is unique over every coefficient ring on xR[[x]] since differences gain at least one in order upon squaring. Modulo 4, F=x+x² solves the equation since C+C²=2C−x. Then F=x+x²+4(C²−xC) modulo 8, since changing the square root by a multiple of four changes its square by a multiple of eight. The claimed powers of two and their successors follow for n>2, taking account of the cancellation at exponent 2. For A393172 write A(x−x²)=x+A(x)^2, equivalently A=C+A(C)^2, with unique fixed point. Modulo two, A=x. Substituting A=x+2B gives B(x+x²)=x² mod 2, so B=C² mod 2; the mod-four support above n=1 is exactly the positive powers 2^j with j≥1. For A389536, A=x+O(x²) and xA=A(x²+2x³): mod 2 one gets A=x by comparing least nonzero coefficient of A−x. Put A=x+2B, so xB=x³+B(x²) mod 2 and B has order at least two. Iteration gives its support at 1+2^j, j≥0. Prior published proofs for all three: https://aiproofwatch.com/updates/five-oeis-congruence-proofs-through-catalan-series-reductions-a476bd18 and https://www.trureturing.org/research/oeis-a393172-catalan-shift-square-mod-four/ ; original pages https://oeis.org/A393171 , https://oeis.org/A393172 , https://oeis.org/A389536 .
* A401159. With H=1+n+binom(n,2), a binary column of height n with at most two ones has H possible supports. For two columns the row constraint is automatic, yielding H². For three columns count H³ and exclude rows present in all three supports. For a specified bad row each column has n choices, for two specified bad rows it has one, and three bad rows are impossible. Inclusion–exclusion gives H³−n⁴+binom(n,2), precisely the conjectured degree-six polynomial. Original https://oeis.org/A401159 ; the two-column identification is recorded at https://oeis.org/A086601 , which cites Richard Mathar's 2014 enumeration (methods predate the new catalog claim).
* A355326 already settled by Sela Fried: https://oeis.org/A355175/a355175.pdf and https://oeis.org/A355326 . A prior proof was explicitly indicated in OEIS; no discovery claim.
