# Audit of the 139-line extension proof

The review compared `139-extension.md` and its `verify_139_profiles.py` against an independent derivation and exact enumeration in `extension139.md` and `checkprofiles.py`. Both proofs give extension to a full order-twelve plane from any 139-block seed. Both use the existing generalized-line-graph classification. Neither constructs the seed.

* **Initial matrix and zero cases.** The pencil bound holds even for a point of degree zero as an upper bound. Positive semidefiniteness follows because the middle rank-139 operator is an orthogonal projection. The residual dimension is exactly 18. Columns of deficit zero have zero Gram norm and all gap entries vanish. If a deficit-one point exists, the pencil adds one line, after which the source 140–156 extensions apply. For higher deficits the identity G²=12G+ttᵀ independently recovers both common-neighbor formulas.
* **Enumeration.** Re-executed the main verifier. It starts with 23 histograms; 9 survive its incidence sieve and 5 survive the sum-vector Cauchy bound. The denominator 26m−E in this bound is the squared norm of the sum of the light Gram vectors. Independent code instead enumerates whether each single point has a multiset of feasible old-line types covering all joined points, and checks cross-type gap counts; it gives precisely the same five histograms. Bounds, loops, symmetry conventions, and the fact that all possible pencil line types are covered were examined.
* **Connectivity.** The two-hop search gives at least 56 lights per component in the first three cases, and a disconnected graph is impossible. Rechecked the 81-vertex and 86-vertex Cauchy contradictions, including 55 lights at the unique deficit-five point, and 27 or 29 at a deficit-three point. The independent rank-and-trace test proves connectivity in all five cases and gives a strictly positive exact surplus even for the 86-vertex near-boundary case.
* **Root classification.** The quoted classification applies to a connected *ordinary simple* graph of least eigenvalue at least −2 on more than 36 vertices, which matches the light graph. A nonempty cocktail-party supplement to an ordinary line graph necessarily introduces a nonadjacent pair with identical open neighborhoods. It cannot occur here because every light has at least 16 light neighbors and any two nonadjacent lights have only four common gap neighbors. Thus a simple root graph exists. Its unsigned incidence matrix has the asserted rank by alternation on a connected graph.
* **Coordinate representation.** Equal Gram matrices identify the spans by a linear isometry. The nonbipartite span fills all 18 coordinates. Odd cycles imply the coordinates are either all integers or all strict half-integers. The latter gives a sum of 18 odd squares modulo 4 after multiplying the squared norm by 4, impossible. Since s is the all-ones vector, integrality gives Σw_i(w_i−1)=0, so the coordinates are binary. This works also for all zero point vectors. The augmented binary matrix is square with column sums 13 and point Gram 12I+J, implying by invertibility both row sums 13 and the same row Gram. This verifies the construction actually gives a plane.
* **Exceptional root.** The 86-light bipartite case also fails directly because some root edge has degree sum 20 in a bipartite graph on ≤19 vertices. For 81 lights, only K₉,₉ is possible. The true heavy vector and the three-row/three-column representative have the same projection and length, so their only choices are the two reflections. A global reflection fixes all lights and s. The two signed ternary words have complementary supports on the 24 heavy points. On each old line their coordinate-sum difference from an integer vector equals a multiple of u/3, giving zero mod 3. Every heavy point lies on ten lines, so a nonzero mixed word has weight ≥14 by elementary pencil counting, and a monochromatic word has weight ≥21. The complementary supports cannot both be nonempty.
* **Independent test against countermodels.** The second proof avoids the ternary code entirely by counting old lines containing exactly two heavy points. For any two fixed affine triple systems of order 9, `checkmodels.py` tries all 256 choices of which parallel classes to reflect. All 256 pass the pairwise binary Gram-product check and 70 pass the heavy-neighbor counts. Only the two globally equivalent canonical choices meet the eight-partner requirement from the old lines. This is a stress test. The theorem does not assume uniqueness of a Steiner triple system or rely on these 256 computations.

No unresolved proof gap surfaced in this audit. The literature comparison here establishes the cited graph theorem; it is not a claim of novelty for the extension threshold. Neither proof has been formally verified in Lean.
