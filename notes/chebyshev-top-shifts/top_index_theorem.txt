A top-shift overlap theorem for unbounded middle gap
==================================================

Hypotheses. Let 7 <= d < e < a < b < c < 2d be distinct odd primes,
let n=deabc, assume
  1/a+1/b+1/c-1/d-1/e = 1/n,
and b-1,c-1 divide n-1. Also assume v=a-e >=502, as supplied by
notes/chebyshev-joint-shifts.md at revision
a6257364d627cf93ab55c836dea48bce831a587b.
No other required or forbidden shift is used in the theorem below.

THEOREM. Set g=gcd(b-1,c-1). Then
  g <= (b-1)/9,
unless all of the following hold:
  b=7g+1, c=10g+1,
  g = 48,138,426, or516 (mod630),
  X=dea=(1610g^3-9409g^2-1167g+9)/9,
  Z=a(d+e)-de=(391g^2-2334g+9)/9.
In particular g <= (b-1)/7 unconditionally under these hypotheses.

This is a proof for every size of g and v. The finite computation below
checks a fixed list of polynomial identities and residue classes; it does
not impose a cutoff on any prime or on v.

1. A preliminary interval inequality
-------------------------------------
We have 3b-2c >=3. Indeed, a<=b-2 and d,e>c/2 imply that the signed
reciprocal sum exceeds
  2/b -3/c +2/b^2.
If 3b-2c<=1, this exceeds -1/(bc)+2/b^2 >1/b^2 >1/n, a contradiction.
The integer 3b-2c is odd, so it is at least3.

Write b=Kg+1,c=Lg+1. Then g is even, gcd(K,L)=1, and
  K<L<3K/2.
Also, d >=(c+1)/2, e>=d+2, a<=b-2 give
  v <= (2K-L)g/2 -4.
Therefore (2K-L)g>=1012, and g is at least the least even integer satisfying
that inequality.

2. A positive integral top-pair parameter
------------------------------------------
Let X=dea, Z=a(d+e)-de, and delta=(L-K)^2. The signed equation is
  X(b+c)-Zbc=1.                                           (1)
The top congruences imply g|(X-1), b|(cX-1), c|(bX-1).
Put
  R=delta-KL(K+L)g-2K^2L^2g^2.
Then
  delta X-R = z g b c                                    (2)
for a positive integer z.

There is no cancellation of L-K or delta in this assertion. Modulo b,
Kc-Lb=K-L gives (L-K)X=-K, hence delta X=-K(L-K).
Reducing R with Kg=-1 gives the same residue. The corresponding residues
modulo c are L(L-K). Modulo g both delta X and R equal delta. The three
moduli g,b,c are pairwise coprime: b,c are distinct primes and each is1
modulo g. Hence their full product divides delta X-R. Finally R<0, so z>0.

Substitution into (1) gives the second exact identity
  delta Z = z g(b+c)+delta-2KL(K+L)g.                     (3)
The two full top shifts give, without cancelling delta,
  K | z c^2 +delta L,   L | z b^2 +delta K.               (4)
For example, multiply cX-1 by delta, substitute (2), divide the resulting
integer polynomial by g, and reduce modulo K. Because Kg divides cX-1,
this is legitimate even if K and g or K and delta share factors. Also
  gcd(c,K)=gcd(b,L)=1,
because the primes b,c exceed K,L.

3. Exact lower and upper envelopes for X
-----------------------------------------
The following two strict bounds depend only on ordering and (1):
  (bc^3-4)/(4(3b-c)) < X < (b^2c^2+2)/(2(2c-b)).          (5)
Here all denominators are positive.

For a directly checkable proof, set T=bc and S=(b+c)/(bc).
Solving (1) for a shows
  X=f(d,e)=de(de-1/T)/(d+e-Sde).
The denominator is positive at the original (d,e). On the rectangle
[c/2,d] x [c/2,e] it remains positive, since it decreases in each variable
and S*c/2>1. Its derivative in the first variable is
  e^2 [d^2(1-Se)+2de-1/T]/(d+e-Sde)^2.
Because both variables lie in [c/2,b), Se<2 and
  d(2e-d) >= d(c-d) > d(c-b) > 1/T,
this derivative is positive; the other derivative is positive by symmetry.
Evaluating at d=e=c/2 proves the lower bound in (5).

For the upper bound solve (1) for e instead:
  X=f2(a,d)=ad(ad+1/T)/(Sad+d-a).
Since Sd>1, this is strictly increasing in a. Increase a to b. For the
associated e(a,d)=(ad+1/T)/(Sad+d-a), its a-derivative is
  [d^2-(Sd-1)/T]/(Sad+d-a)^2 >0,
so the resulting e remains greater than d. With a=b fixed, decreasing d
toward c/2 increases e, because
  de/dd = -[b^2+(Sb+1)/T]/(Sbd+d-b)^2 <0.
Throughout that segment e>d, and this is exactly the condition that
  df2/dd = b^2[d^2(Sb+1)-2bd-1/T]/(Sbd+d-b)^2
be negative. The denominator is positive even at d=c/2, where it is c-b/2.
Thus f2(b,c/2)>X; its value is the upper expression in (5).

4. The finite index cover, uniform for all g
--------------------------------------------
If K<=8, K<L<3K/2 and gcd(K,L)=1 leave exactly these ten rows.
The lower bound on g comes from (2K-L)g>=1012 and evenness.
Substituting (5) into (2) gives the following convenient uniform bounds.
Put H=2L-K and delta=(L-K)^2. Then
  delta L^2/[4(3K-L)] < z
    < delta KL/(2H)
       +[2KL+delta(2L^2-K^2)/(2H^2)]/g +delta/(Hg^2).      (6)
The upper bound decreases as g grows. Here is an algebraic verification.
Writing E=-delta+KL(K+L)g+2K^2L^2g^2, equation (2) reads
  z=(delta X+E)/(gbc).
We have E>delta and E/(gbc)<2KL/g. In the lower envelope for X, the
negative correction is delta/[gbc(3b-c)], which is smaller than E/(gbc).
Also
  (3K-L)c^2-L^2g(3b-c)
    =2L(3K-2L)g+3K-L>0.
This proves the lower half of (6). For the upper half, polynomial division
and positive denominators give
  bc/[2g(Hg+1)]
    < KL/(2H)+(2L^2-K^2)/(2H^2g)+1/(2Hg^2).
The remaining term 1/[gbc(Hg+1)] is less than1/(2Hg^2), since bc>2.
Together with E/(gbc)<2KL/g, this proves (6).

Substituting g_min into the decreasing upper bound, and using the constant
lower bound, gives the listed strict interval for integer z.

All ten rational comparisons in (6) are exact and are checked by the
standard-library verifier. For example the tightest upper comparison,
K=5,L=7,g_min=338, is 18484891/2313441<8.
The residue exclusions can be checked individually as follows.
* (3,4): z=1 would require c^2=-1 mod3.
* (4,5): z=1 would require c^2=-1 mod4.
* (5,6): z=2 contradicts the L-test modulo3.
* (5,7): z=7 contradicts the L-test modulo7.
* (6,7): the K-test requires z=5 mod6, outside1,...,4.
* (7,8): the L-test requires z=1 mod8, outside2,3.
* (7,9): the L-test permits only z=8 or11 in7,...,12. Both are quadratic
  residues modulo7, whereas the K-test requires z*c^2=-1 mod7.
* (7,10): the L-test leaves z=23. The K- and L-tests then give the four
  residues g=6,26,48,68 mod70.
* (8,9): the K-test requires z=7 mod8, outside2,...,4.
* (8,11): the K-test leaves z=21 or29. The L-test would require b^2=6 or7
  modulo11, respectively; neither is a quadratic residue.
In these uses b and c are units for the relevant modulus, as established
following (4).

An independent polynomial certificate verifies the same interval bounds
without using the estimates in (6). For its row, put
E=-delta+KL(K+L)g+2K^2L^2g^2 and b=Kg+1,c=Lg+1.
If the interval is lo<z<hi, define
  P_upper=2 hi g b c(2c-b)-delta(b^2c^2+2)-2(2c-b)E,
  P_lower=delta(bc^3-4)+4(3b-c)E-4 lo g b c(3b-c).
For each row, substitute g=g_min+x. Every coefficient of each polynomial
is nonnegative and its constant term is positive. Thus both polynomial
inequalities hold for every real x>=0. The accompanying standard-library
verifier constructs these polynomials and checks every coefficient.
It also enumerates all even residues g modulo lcm(2,K,L) for (4), imposing
gcd(c,K)=gcd(b,L)=1. This exhausts the residue question at unbounded g.

5. The surviving one-parameter family
---------------------------------------
For K=7,L=10,z=23, equations (2)-(3) become the X,Z polynomials in the
statement. Integrality of Z forces 3|g. Writing g=3t gives
  X=4830t^3-9409t^2-389t+1,
  Z=391t^2-778t+1.
Then g|(X-1) holds exactly when t=1 mod3, or g=3 mod9.
Combining this with g=6,26,48,68 mod70 gives exactly
  g=48,138,426,516 mod630.
Conversely these progressions make X,Z integral and satisfy (1) and both
full top cofactor congruences
  cX=1 mod b(b-1),   bX=1 mod c(c-1).
This last sufficiency is a statement about the two top tests only.
The verifier proves it symbolically by dividing the corresponding
polynomials in t for g=g0+630t, obtaining integer polynomial quotients.

An explicit top-pair near miss has
  g=1776, b=12433, c=17761,
  X=998804996977, Z=136570849.
Here b,c and X are prime by exact trial division. The pair identity and
both top cofactor congruences hold exactly. Since X is itself prime, it
cannot equal a product dea of three distinct primes; this is NOT a
counterexample to the claim. It demonstrates that the top restrictions,
even with these precise invariants, do not by themselves give a contradiction.

6. Remaining work and limitations
-----------------------------------
The theorem leaves (i) K>=9 and (ii) the displayed K=7,L=10 family.
A full solution would have to rule out a factorization X=dea with the
ordering, invariant Z=a(d+e)-de, and the remaining required/forbidden
shifts in those configurations. No bound on unbounded v, or proof that
these configurations are empty, has been obtained here.

The proof did not replace M*lcm(b-1,c-1) by separate divisibility tests.
Instead it used the exact original signed equation and original top
cofactor congruences. All product-divisibility steps explicitly used
pairwise coprimality of g,b,c, which is valid under the prime hypotheses.
No assumption about gcd(M,lcm(b-1,c-1)) was made.
