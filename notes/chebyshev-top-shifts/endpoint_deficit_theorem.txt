CHEBYSHEV ENDPOINT DEFICIT: AN UNBOUNDED-PARAMETER EXCLUSION

Hypotheses. Let 7 <= d < e < a < b < c < 2d be distinct primes,
n=deabc, and assume the signed reciprocal identity together with the
required shifts d+1,c-1 dividing n-1. Write k=2d-c, X=eab, and
G=gcd(d+1,c-1)=gcd(d+1,k+3). For the assertion k!=1, also assume the
forbidden shift d-1 does not divide n-1.

THEOREM. Put T=k^2(k+1)^2(k+2)^2. Every candidate has

  d < k + G [2T+6k^2+12k+4],       G | k+3.

Together with the exact finite certificate described below, every full
candidate satisfies k>=9. In particular, the endpoint deficit 2d-c must
tend to infinity at least as a constant times d^(1/7). If G is bounded,
the exponent improves to 1/6. The middle gap v=a-e is unrestricted.
These statements do not settle the full interval c<2d.

1. Local cofactor equations, with no cancellation of a shared factor.
The signed identity gives cX=-1 (mod d) and dX=1 (mod c). The required
shifts give the same respective congruences modulo d+1 and c-1. Hence

  cX=-1 (mod d(d+1)),    dX=1 (mod c(c-1)).

In particular,

  d | kX-1,
  d+1 | (k+2)X-1,
  c | kX-2,
  c-1 | (k+1)X-2.

The final two follow by multiplication by 2, since 2d=c+k.
No inverse of k or k+1 is taken here.

2. The interpolation polynomial.
Let C=24k^2+48k+16 and

 P(x)=-C x^3+(12k^3+8k^2-24k-8)x^2
      +(2k^4+20k^3+42k^2+28k+8)x+k(k+1)^2(k+2)^2.

Let L(x)=P(x)/T. Its values at x=0,-1,k/2,(k+1)/2 are respectively
1/k,1/(k+2),2/k,2/(k+1). These are polynomial identities after clearing
the indicated denominators. Also P(x) is a polynomial in 2x with integer
coefficients: the cubic coefficient is divisible by 8, the quadratic by
4, and the linear by 2.

Set F=T X-P(d). The four divisibilities in step 1 now imply respectively

  d|F, d+1|F, c|F, c-1|F.

For example, write P(d)=Ptilde(2d), reduce modulo c or c-1, and use
Ptilde(k)=2T/k or Ptilde(k+1)=2T/(k+1). All displayed quotients T/k,
T/(k+1),T/(k+2) are integers. Thus this argument does not cancel a
nonunit modulo an even shift.

Among d,d+1,c,c-1 only the two shifts can share a prime. Indeed d,c are
distinct primes, d+1<c, and d<c-1<2d, so gcd(d,c-1)=1. Consequently

 lcm(d,d+1,c,c-1) = d(d+1)c(c-1)/G.

It follows that

  G T [X-L(d)]/[d(d+1)c(c-1)]

is an integer. The factor G is essential and has been retained.

3. Positivity, including the possible zero remainder.
For k>=3 and x>=k one has

  0 < -L(x) < (C/T)x^3.

For the upper bound, the three non-leading coefficients of P are positive.
For the lower bound put x=k+z, z>=0. The coefficients of -P(k+z), in
descending powers of z, are

 C,
 60k^3+136k^2+72k+8,
 46k^4+108k^3+54k^2-12k-8,
 k(9k^4+14k^3-15k^2-32k-12).

They are positive for k>=3; alternatively the exact verifier expands
k=3+w and verifies nonnegative coefficients and positive constants.
Since X>0, the integer in step 2 is strictly positive. In particular
there is no zero-remainder exception.

4. The bound.
Since X=eab<8d^3, step 3 gives

 d(d+1)c(c-1) <= G T[X-L(d)] < G(8T+C)d^3.

Also (d+1)(c-1)>dc, since c-d-1>0. Therefore

 (2d-k)^2 = c^2 < G(8T+C)d.

Expanding and discarding the positive k^2 gives

 d < k + G(8T+C)/4 = k + G[2T+6k^2+12k+4].

Finally G=gcd(d+1,k+3), so G<=k+3. These inequalities hold for unbounded
k and v. If k=1, then c-1=2(d-1), so the required c-1 divisibility would
force the forbidden d-1 divisibility. Thus k>=3.

5. Complete finite reductions for k=3,5,7.
For each such k, the preceding theorem gives the finite range

 d <= k+(k+3)[2k^2(k+1)^2(k+2)^2+6k^2+12k+4]-1.

The resulting maximum d values are 43,766; 707,316; and 5,084,146.
For each prime d>k with c=2d-k prime, retain the sharper actual-G bound.
The two cofactor congruences in step 1 determine all possible X by the
generalized CRT, whose period is d(d+1)c(c-1)/G. Enumerate all its
representatives d^3<X<c^3. This interval includes every eab. Exact
trial division then tests whether X is three distinct primes in (d,c).

The complete results are:
 k=3: 877 endpoint pairs, 3 cofactor representatives, zero eligible triples.
 k=5: 4249 endpoint pairs, 2 cofactor representatives, zero eligible triples.
 k=7: 18717 endpoint pairs, 2 cofactor representatives, zero eligible triples.

All seven cofactor representatives (d,c,X) are
 (13,23,8641), (23,43,55829), (521,1039,148201229),
 (11,17,2275), (47,89,530743),
 (43,79,226561), (14629,29251,3153054217219).

The first, second and fifth X values are prime. The remaining values
have, respectively, a factor 11<521; a factor 5<11; factorization
71*3191 with 3191>79; and a factor 193<14629. Thus none can be eab.
The certificate uses an ordinary Eratosthenes sieve and exact integer
CRT and trial division. It does not assume the conjecture, use probable
prime tests, or impose a numerical cutoff on v.

Run:
 python3 verify_chebyshev_endpoint_deficit.py

This proves k>=9 at every prime size, while leaving configurations with
k>=9 and d below the displayed polynomial bound unexcluded.
