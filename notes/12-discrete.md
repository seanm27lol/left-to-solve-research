# Graph constructions, walks, and counting

## mo-332658 · exact transitive domination number

For each k≥1 a tournament T_k on 3^(k−1) vertices has minimum transitive dominating set of size k. Define T_1 to consist of one vertex, and form T_{k+1} from three disjoint copies of T_k, with all edges between blocks oriented in a directed triangle. A dominating set meets at least two blocks, since the predecessor of a single occupied block is undominated. A transitive subtournament meets at most two blocks, since a choice from three blocks spans a directed triangle. Thus exactly two are occupied, say A→B. Its intersection with A must dominate A internally. Conversely choose a minimum transitive dominating set in A and one vertex in B. A dominates B, and B dominates the unused block, establishing the recursion t(T_{k+1})=1+t(T_k). Independent existence route: for each fixed k, random tournaments have no ordinary dominating set of size k−1 with positive probability for large N (union bound ≤binom(N,k−1)(1−2^{−(k−1)})^{N−k+1}). Every tournament has a transitive dominating set by greedy out-neighborhood removal (successive picked vertices must instead lie among the in-neighbors of all earlier choices; they beat the earlier choices, giving transitivity). For any v, t(T)≤1+t(T−v): if v is not dominated by an optimal transitive set in T−v, v beats it all and may be added as source. Along a chain of induced subtournaments from size 1 to N, t consequently cannot jump upward past k. Bukh suggested a different construction in a source comment. https://mathoverflow.net/questions/332658/minimum-transitive-dominating-subtournament

## mo-167669 · unimodal distance spheres

Every finite connected regular graph of diameter 2 has shell vector (1,d,n−d−1), which is unimodal since d≥1. The complements of C_n, n≥6, supply examples that fail distance regularity: adjacent vertices at original cyclic distances two and three have respectively n−5 and n−6 common neighbors. Every original consecutive pair has a common neighbor in the complement, so its diameter really is 2. An independent unbounded-diameter family consists of prisms C_m □ K₂ for m≥7. Its distance polynomial from any vertex is (1+z) times P_m(z), with P_m=1+2z+…+2z^h for odd m=2h+1, and P_m=1+2z+…+2z^{h−1}+z^h for even m=2h. The resulting coefficients are unimodal (adjacent two-term sums); displacement (2,0) has one common neighbor with the basepoint, displacement (1,1) has two, so the distance-two intersection number differs. Source comment speculates about random regular graphs; the deterministic families above answer the stated existence/class request. https://mathoverflow.net/questions/167669/regular-graphs-with-unimodal-subdegrees-that-are-not-distance-regular

## mo-359899 · black/white board counts, including seams and exact weight

For n≥2, encode each row by its dark set a⊆{1,…,n}. Let E_h be adjacent column pairs (include {1,n} for horizontal periodicity). Set C_ab=1 exactly if each pair in E_h intersects a∪b; set w_a=z^{|a|}, M_ab=C_ab w_b. With open vertical boundary the weight polynomial is w^T M^{n−1}1, and with periodic vertical boundary it is tr(M^n). Every board has precisely its own ordered row word. Each adjacency tests all squares crossing that seam, and each row contributes its weight once, including the closing transfer. Evaluate at 1 for totals and extract [z^k] for exactly k black squares. This covers either, both or neither gluing without quotienting by translations. Convention for n=1: if forbidden squares require four distinct board cells, there are two boards. Independent formula with the same boundary choices: for event set Q of forbidden unit squares, count of weight k is Σ_{J⊆Q}(−1)^|J| binom(n²−|∪_{s∈J}s|, k). Checked by brute force: n=2 gives 15 for all three boundary types; n=3 gives open 417, cylinder 380, torus 334; n=4 gives 42176,37872,33159. On the 3-torus exactly 6 weight-3 and 81 weight-4 boards. The original comments credit Simon Cowell's prior closed-box matrix enumeration, https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03580, and refer to OEIS A139810, A255936. ## mo-209264 · incomparable under two graph relations

Let G consist of a triangle plus a pendant path with nine edges; 12 vertices and 12 edges. Let H=μ(C5): originals a_i form a 5-cycle, each twin b_i is adjacent to a_{i−1},a_{i+1}, and apex w is adjacent to all five b_i. H has 11 vertices and 20 edges. No triangle uses w (its neighbors are independent) or b_i (its two original neighbors are nonadjacent), and the original C5 has none. It is not 3-colorable: in a putative 3-coloring, replace the color of each original vertex colored like w with the color of its own twin. Adjacent originals cannot both have w's color and each twin is adjacent to the other original, so this gives a 2-coloring of C5. Thus G cannot map to H (triangle), and H cannot map to G (chromatic number). Minor operations on simple graphs never increase vertex or edge counts. Thus G cannot be a minor of H (vertices) and H cannot be a minor of G (edges). This addresses both symmetric requirements added by the author after a comment. Alternate construction: take G a triangle plus arbitrarily many leaves, H any fixed 4-chromatic triangle-free graph; G is unicyclic, all minors have cycle rank at most one while H contains a subgraph of minimum degree three. https://mathoverflow.net/questions/209264/graphs-with-no-homomorphism-and-no-minor-relation

## mo-324546 · number of Hasse edges of a skew Young interval

Write K(a,b) for the number of partitions x with a≤x≤b coordinatewise, with K(empty,empty)=1. For partitions a,b of length d (pad with zeros), the usual Kreweras determinant is K(a,b)=det[binom(b_j−a_i+1,j−i+1)]_{i,j=1}^d, with binomial zero for invalid arguments. The edge count is the sum, over i and μ_i<j≤λ_i, of K(max(μ_{<i},j),λ_{<i}) K(μ_{>i},min(λ_{>i},j−1)); max/min are componentwise. Count each edge at its higher partition with marked removable corner (i,j). Row i must equal j, preceding rows must be at least j, and following rows at most j−1. Those two intervals now vary independently. This directly supplies a finite sum of products of determinants; no claim to a single determinant is needed. Second viewpoint: in any finite order-ideal lattice the number of covering edges is Σ_p |J(P_incomparable(p))|, since every strict predecessor of a toggleable element p is forced in and its strict successors forced out. For Young diagrams the incomparable part splits into the described two components. Exact enumeration of all 490 intervals in the 3×4 box agreed. The OP notes a different corner-sum reference (Theorem 3.4); Stanley's comment interprets the count by plane partitions with precisely one part 2. Original and bibliography https://mathoverflow.net/questions/324546/formula-for-number-of-edges-in-hasse-diagram-of-youngs-lattice-interval and http://www.numdam.org/item/BURO_1965__6__9_0/ .

## mo-426479 · first-return walks on the honeycomb

The OP clarified the degree-three honeycomb graph. If a_n counts closed walks of length 2n and f_n counts first returns, decomposing uniquely at first return gives a_0=1 and a_n=Σ_{j=1}^n f_j a_{n-j}; consequently F=1−1/A for ordinary series in half-length, F(0)=0. For a second way of computing the input, the directions on odd steps are u₁,u₂,u₃, on even steps their negatives. The only linear dependence is u₁+u₂+u₃=0 and both count vectors sum to n; closure therefore requires identical direction multiplicities. Thus a_n=Σ_{p+q+r=n}(n!/(p!q!r!))², giving f_1,…,f_4=3,6,30,180. Taylor's 2022 comment already gives exactly the renewal relation and references A002893. https://mathoverflow.net/questions/426479/random-walk-on-hexagonal-lattice-first-return-to-the-origin and https://oeis.org/A002893

## mo-277790 · longest word with distinct length-m windows

A word of length L≥m has L−m+1 windows, so L≤q^m+m−1. De Bruijn's digraph has all (m−1)-words as vertices and a directed edge for every m-word joining prefix to suffix; each vertex has in- and out-degree q, and is connected to any target by appending its digits. An Euler circuit consequently uses each of the q^m distinct edges exactly once. Write the initial vertex and append each edge's last symbol. Hierholzer's algorithm constructs an equality word in O(q^m) edge operations. For m=1 use the single empty vertex and q distinct loops; q=1 gives L=m. The proposed upper bound in the question is too small when m≥3. This is the classical de Bruijn result and is explicitly answered in the thread by Emil Jeřábek. https://mathoverflow.net/questions/277790/longest-string-with-all-unique-substrings

## mo-427001 · difference sets with a fixed prime budget

Under the intended condition on all nonzero differences, let p_1=2,p_2=3,… be the primes. Then f(r)=p_{r+1}. For the upper bound let ℓ be the first prime excluded from the set of r permitted primes. Distinct elements of S must give different residues modulo ℓ, so |S|≤ℓ≤p_{r+1}. The interval S={0,…,p_{r+1}−1} realizes equality, since each nonzero absolute difference is positive and less than p_{r+1}. This sharpens the lower-bound request and already appears in comments by Gerry Myerson and Ivan Meir. https://mathoverflow.net/questions/427001/maximum-size-of-difference-sets-with-a-bounded-number-of-prime-divisors

## Catalogue corrections in the literature

The API search https://lefttosolve.com/api/problems?q=graph&limit=8 returned both of the following with solved:false, kind:unformalized. These have already been treated in the research literature. Full-paper references and locator IDs are given here.

* wp-implicit-graph-conjecture. Hatami & Hatami, Theorem 1.4, for any small δ>0 build a hereditary class of factorial speed needing adjacency labels of length Ω(n^(1/2−δ)); labels of logarithmic length are impossible. Decoder labels would induce a polynomial-size universal graph (append vertex IDs if labels coincide). Their proof counts carefully chosen sparse bipartite graphs: small induced subgraphs have bounded degeneracy, keeping the hereditary closure factorial, while a counting bound excludes every short-label scheme. Dense unrestricted random graphs would lose the hereditary speed bound. https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13198 and https://doi.org/10.1109/FOCS54457.2022.00109
* wp-is-the-line-graph-of-a-non-word-representable-graph-always-non-word-representabl. Mozhui, Dwary & Krishna, Theorem 3, show the line graph of the Mycielski graph on every odd cycle of length ≥5 is word-representable, while the original Mycielski graph is not. The first case is μ(C5) on 11 vertices, whose line graph has 20 vertices. This disproves the universal implication. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03339 (arXiv preprint).

Independent finite check of the smallest line-graph witness: the twenty edges of μ(C5) give 2²⁰ orientations. Warshall transitive closure on 11 vertices found 167400 acyclic orientations. Every one contains an oriented edge u→v with another directed path u→…→v: intersect the reachability set of u with the predecessor set of v. Since μ(C5) has no triangles, such a path violates semitransitivity. For its line graph, denote a mixed edge by (i,j') for original vertex i or apex i=0 and twin j'; index cycle vertices 1,…,5. Put all 15 mixed edges first in descending i and then ascending j, followed by the cycle edges (4,5),(3,4),(2,3),(1,5),(1,2). Orient adjacencies by this total order. Enumerating its 295 nonempty increasing directed paths found exactly eleven with at least four vertices and adjacent ends; all eleven had every chord. This is an independently checked ordered certificate of semitransitivity, using the known equivalence between word-representability and semitransitive orientability. Implementations the accompanying verification scripts, chebyshev_search.cpp (intermediate files).
