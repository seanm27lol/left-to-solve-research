# Three unit fractions and graceful tree labelings

## Sierpiński's three Egyptian fractions

**Catalog target.** ID `SierpinskiEgyptianFractionConjecture.sierpinski_egyptian_fraction_conjecture`, explicitly `kind=open`. For every $n\ge2$, find positive integers $x,y,z$ with $5/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$. The target permits equal denominators, unlike a historical variant. A literature search including the 2025–26 work of Pomerance and Weingartner found no full proof for fixed numerator 5.

**Constructive partial theorem.** It suffices to handle primes $p\equiv1\pmod{60}$. Scaling a representation for $p$ by $n/p$ solves every multiple $n$. For the residue argument, let $t=\lceil n/5\rceil$, $N=nt$; after removing $1/t$ the remainder is $(5t-n)/N$. For residues $4,3\bmod5$ the numerators are $1,2$, so split the former unit fraction or use two copies of $1/N$. If $n\equiv2\bmod5$, then $n=5t-3$, $N\equiv2t^2\bmod3$. When $3\mid N$, $3/N$ is a unit fraction which splits in two. Otherwise $N\equiv2\bmod3$, so setting $h=(N+1)/3$ gives $3/N=1/h+1/(Nh)$. For $5\mid n$ use $1/u=1/(2u)+1/(3u)+1/(6u)$ with $u=n/5$. This includes the small prime $2$ in the residue-2 case. Any prime left is $1\bmod5$. If it is $3\bmod4$, take $5/p=1/p+4/p$ and use $4/p=1/h+1/(ph)$, $h=(p+1)/4$.

**Factorization route and its limit.** The remaining primes are $1\bmod20$. With $t=(p+4)/5$, $N=pt\equiv1\bmod4$, splitting the remainder $4/N$ is equivalent to $(4y-N)(4z-N)=N^2$. It works precisely when $N$ has a prime factor $q\equiv3\bmod4$: use $4y-N=q$, $4z-N=N^2/q$, both $3\bmod4$. Conversely, a positive divisor $3\bmod4$ must include such a prime. In particular, if $p\equiv41\bmod60$ then $3\mid t$; this leaves only primes $p\equiv1\bmod60$ (the residue $21\bmod60$ is divisible by 3). Some of the remaining primes are also covered whenever $(p+4)/5$ has the requisite factor. The fixed choice $t$ provably fails for $p=61$, since $N=61\cdot13$ has only $1\bmod4$ prime factors; changing the first denominator gives $5/61=1/14+1/95+1/81130$. The unresolved step is a choice of first denominator that handles every remaining prime. The residue coverage is a partial result, with no novelty claim.

## Graceful tree conjecture

**Catalog target.** ID `GracefulLabeling.graceful_tree_conjecture`, explicitly `kind=open`. Label the $m+1$ vertices of any finite tree bijectively with $0,\ldots,m$ so the edge differences exhaust $1,\ldots,m$. Letzter, Pokrovskiy and Williams prove an asymptotic lower bound on the number of distinct differences. Their theorem permits missing differences.

**First route, exact constructive subclass.** In a caterpillar choose a spine path so all other vertices are leaves touching the spine. Start its first vertex at zero. When the current spine label came from the low end, give its unused leaf neighbors the largest available labels successively, then give the *next* spine vertex the next largest. When the current label came from the high end, do the same using the smallest remaining labels. Stop after labeling the leaves of the final spine vertex. At each step the unused labels constitute an interval: at a low-end parent the first new edge has difference equal to the interval length and successive edges decrease the difference by one; the same holds reflected at a high-end parent. Indeed the parent is one below the interval initially, or one above it after a switch; the final child of a batch is adjacent in label value to the new interval on its side. Since each batch consumes as many labels as edges, differences run once through $m,m-1,\ldots,1$. This reproves Rosa's caterpillar theorem; the one-vertex tree is immediate.

**Second route, failed extension lemma with a complete finite test.** A vertex labeled zero can take $b$ new leaves by assigning them $m+1,\ldots,m+b$ and preserving all old labels. Any fixed attachment vertex need not permit zero. Take vertices $0,\ldots,5$ and edges $01,02,03,34,45$ (indices denote vertices, not labels). Exhaust all $6!=720$ vertex-label permutations, keeping a permutation exactly when its five absolute edge differences form $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$. There are 12 survivors, and their vertices assigned zero form exactly $\{0,1,2,4,5\}$. Thus attaching at vertex 3 cannot be certified by arbitrarily rerooting a graceful labeling at zero. This agrees with the known non-0-rotatable caterpillar example. A full solution needs to handle branches off a spine without these conflicting label requirements; finite checks and asymptotically many differences do not do so.
