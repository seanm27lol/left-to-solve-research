# Independent mathematical audit of MO 334087

The argument in the eigenvalue manuscript proves the stated real symmetric result for every nonempty block. The variational construction and its final eigenvalue comparison survived the following checks.

1. Set s=n−m(k−1) and r=s−m+1, so s−r+1=m. For n>mk and k>1, n>s>m, n<ks, and r≥2. When n=mk the nonempty block is exactly the mth eigenvalue.
2. An n by s full-spark isometry has all row squared norms strictly between zero and one. A norm of one would put the corresponding e_i in S and make every one of the n−1 other rows orthogonal to a fixed nonzero coefficient vector, although some s of those rows must span. Density follows by finite real polynomial avoidance prior to orthonormalization. The minimum constants a,b are over a finite collection of invertible row submatrices and are strictly positive. For the s smallest quadratic forms, Hadamard gives product ≥a det T and the minimum singular value gives sum ≥b tr T. Consequently the other forms are at least b/s, allowing F→∞ at every singular limit on the normalized compact cone.
3. Scaling T to λmax(T)=1 decreases F, so a minimizer on that slice globally minimizes F on 0<T≤I. This justifies testing a negative rank-one perturbation in any direction and a positive rank-one perturbation in the whole strictly subunit spectral space V. Semidefiniteness turns v^TGv=0 into Gv=0. Invariance of V under T^{1/2} now gives E|_V=kI. The strict quadratic inequality on V^perp follows from q_i<1, the isometry identity, and T|_{V^perp}=I. Since V^perp is nonempty, tr E exceeds k dim V+s−dim V. Thus dim V≤m−1, as required. The Gram-matrix step and the scalar trace rescaling both preserve the target operator inequalities.
4. Every resulting Q obeys 0≤Q≤P, so Q annihilates the orthogonal complement of the top-s subspace. Its diagonal entries in an eigenbasis of H on that subspace give weights in [0,1] of total r. Their smallest possible weighted sum is λ_m+…+λ_s, using descending order, including repeated values. Passing from the generic to arbitrary subspace by subsequence is permitted by bounded operator norm and closed semidefinite order.
5. When n=mk, discard all zero-diagonal rows of A (entire rows vanish by positivity). The normalized h by h correlation matrix is PSD and has trace h≤mk. It cannot have m eigenvalues strictly greater than k. Congruence and reinstatement of zeros preserve positive inertia. The case k=1 is the lower tail of a mean-zero ordered list. When k=n,m=1 only the previously covered endpoint remains.
6. The original thread by Victor Reis and Christian Remling reports proofs of m=1 and asks about m>1. Nikhil Bansal, *On a Generalization of Iterated and Randomized Rounding*, Theory of Computing 20(6) (2024), Thm 2.5, attributes an important antecedent to Bansal–Garg (2017). On an s-dimensional subspace it constructs a supported positive matrix U with U≤k diag U, diagonal entries ≤1, and tr U≥s−n/k. The present certificate additionally bounds U≤P_S and proves the larger trace r=s−m+1. The earlier theorem as stated does not yield those bounds. Its standard correlation-matrix inertia observation is sufficient at n=mk. Related radial isotropic position gives context for the variational objective, but no earlier proof of the whole precise interior sum was located in an exact-title, theorem-term and author search. Priority remains undetermined.
