# Extremal graphs, sets of multiples, and necklaces

## Erdős 488 — How much can the density of a set of multiples grow?

Live catalog (kind open): https://lefttosolve.com/p/Erdos488.erdos_488 . Original: https://www.erdosproblems.com/488 . Declan Gessel's September counterexample: https://gist.github.com/declangessel/d8e15e5ff1b6c7e99e3d86d7c66f1d08 . Alicien B.'s second dyadic-band proof: https://aixiv.online/pdf/2611.02020v2 . Independent replay and attribution for C. Benefield's stronger unbounded-ratio result and D. Z. Liu's work: https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/day/488-gessel-counterexample-refereed-and-made-explicit . This is an independently reconstructed generalization of the smooth-band method, and the unboundedness conclusion already appears in prior work.

**Exact target.** For finite nonempty A of integers at least two, write B(y) for the number of positive integers ≤y divisible by a member of A. Is B(m)/m < 2 B(n)/n for all m>n≥max A? No. Indeed, the quotient of the two densities is unbounded across such choices, even for primitive A.

**Proof.** Let P=2^L with L≥2. Write H(t) for the number of odd P-smooth positive integers ≤t and Q for the product of the odd primes ≤P. With r the number of such primes, H(P^j) ≤ (1+Lj)^r by encoding the prime exponents. Consequently, for some j≥1, H(P^{j+1}) ≤ 2H(P^j), since otherwise H(P^j) would grow at least exponentially in j. Set X=P^j, n=PX, and let A consist of all P-smooth integers in [X,2X). It is nonempty (X is included) and primitive. An integer counted by B(n) equals ac for a∈A and c≤P, so is P-smooth. Its odd part is at most n, and a given odd part occurs with at most L+1 different 2-adic valuations in [X,PX]. Thus B(n) ≤ 2(L+1)H(X).

For each odd P-smooth w≤X choose the unique power-of-two multiple a_w of w in [X,2X). Take m=(2n)!Q. Among its multiples a_w u≤m, exactly m φ(Q)/(a_w Q) have gcd(u,Q)=1, because Q divides m/a_w. The classes for distinct w are disjoint: w is recovered as the product of the full prime powers of the odd primes ≤P in a_w u. Since a_w<2X, B(m)/m>H(X)φ(Q)/(2XQ). The density ratio therefore exceeds P φ(Q)/[4(L+1)Q]. The prime product φ(Q)/Q is at least the product of 1−1/j over all odd j from 3 to P−1. For j=2i+1, (2i/(2i+1))²≥(2i−1)/(2i+1), and the latter factors telescope. Thus φ(Q)/Q≥1/√(P−1). The ratio exceeds √P/[4(L+1)]. For L=16 this exceeds 256/68>2, and it tends to infinity with L. This gives the actual negation with all integer endpoints and positive denominators.

**Second route and obstruction.** For one generator a, floor(n/a)>n/(2a) whenever n≥a. Arbitrarily large sets are essential to this proof: simply adding such individual bounds double-counts overlaps among the multiples. The smooth cofactor construction arranges many overlaps at n and an explicit disjoint collection at m. A direct inclusion-exclusion computation using lcm(A) is exact for any fixed input, but cannot turn checking small generators into a universal statement.

## Erdős 573 — triangle- and quadrilateral-free extremal graphs

Live catalog (kind open): https://lefttosolve.com/p/Erdos573.erdos_573 . Original: https://www.erdosproblems.com/573 . Peer-reviewed earlier lower bound: Jie Ma and Tianchi Yang, https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2025.10100 . Counterexample preprint by Will Howe, Grant Huffman, Jensen Kohlmeyer, and Liam Kruer (November 2026): https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00749v1 . The paper's own claim concerns the limiting ratio, and its posted Lean artifacts are at https://github.com/Ghuffy1/erdos-573/tree/75b87c642d5800f5ce45564236ecf065820c9eb1 . No claim of priority, peer review, or new Lean verification for this adaptation.

**Exact target**: Does ex(N,{C3,C4})/(N/2)^{3/2} tend to 1? No. We rechecked the preprint’s construction and give a quantitative improvement on its printed constant. The original takes J={1,…,10}. Take J={1,…,11} instead. The limiting lower bound changes from 1.000045214335… to 1.000096832345…. This is an improvement on that parameter choice, not a claim that it beats all work in the literature.

**Construction and short-cycle audit.** Set p=659 and q=p^h, h≥1. Take a nonzero linear functional τ:F_q→F_p (the finite-field trace works) with fibers of size q/p. Let I={1,…,7}. The seventh-power subgroup H⊂F_p^× has order 94 and contains −1. The 94th powers of 1,…,7 modulo 659 are 1,307,144,12,389,55,410, so I represents its cosets. Pick 47 representatives μ for H/{±1}. Let U={v:τ(v)∈I}. Root slopes are a≠0 satisfying τ(1/a)∉J; positive and negative leaf slopes in block μ are b=1/(μv), c=−1/(μw) for v,w∈U. Reciprocal traces lie in the disjoint sets ±μI, so each leaf slope determines its part. Each slope has q intercepts. A root (a,x) joins a leaf (d,y) if y−x=ad. Leaves (b,y),(c,z) of one block join precisely when z−y=μbc and τ(v+w)∈J. Tags distinguish root and leaf vertices.

A triangle would give a(c−b)=μbc, hence 1/a=v+w. The trace requirements are incompatible. The graph of part labels is a center joined to the two endpoints of each edge of a matching, so a four-cycle would repeat a part at opposite vertices. Two distinct root vertices determine at most one common leaf by (a−a')d=x'−x. Two positive leaves of unequal slope determine at most one common root and at most one negative leaf by subtracting the respective adjacency equations. If both exist then a=−μc=1/w, ruled out by τ(w)∈I⊂J. For two negative leaves the same equations instead force a=μb=1/v; identical slopes with different intercepts have no common neighbor. Thus neither short cycle exists.

**Counts.** There are (p−11)q/p−1 root slopes and (p−1)q/p leaf slopes. Of the 49 ordered pairs of I-elements, 43 have sum in J (the counts of sums 12,13,14 are 3,2,1). Each pair of allowed trace classes contributes (q/p)^2 slope pairs, each supporting a perfect matching of size q. Hence N=(1306/659)q²−q and E=(428405/659²)q³−(658/659)q², since (659−11)658+47·43=428405. The square of the limiting ratio is 8·428405²/(659·1306³), whose numerator exceeds its denominator by 284306256. A subsequence suffices to disprove convergence to 1. The exact extremal function is separate. Earlier additive lower bounds of order N^{5/4} alone cannot distinguish the leading constants. This check validates all h, unlike one finite high-density graph.

**Optimization within the stated template.** With p=659 and I={1,…,7} fixed, J={1,…,11} maximizes the limiting ratio over all J⊇I in the prime field. For a fixed t=|J|, maximize T, the number of accepted ordered sums from I², since the root contribution and number of vertices depend only on t in the leading term. Sums 8 to 14 have multiplicities 7,6,…,1, while the mandatory sums ≤7 contribute 21. The optimal T for t=7,…,14 is 21,28,34,39,43,46,48,49. The ratio squared is 8[(p−t)(p−1)+47T]²/[p(2p−1−t)³]. Comparing these eight rational numbers by cross multiplication gives the unique maximum at t=11. For t≥14 this expression strictly decreases: writing X=(p−1)(p−t)+47·49 and V=2p−1−t, the logarithmic derivative has the sign of 3X−2(p−1)V=3·47·49−(p−1)(p+t−2)<0. The proof of the absence of short cycles uses only I⊆J and the trace test. Including zero in J changes at most one root slope relative to the lower-order count, so the same leading formula applies. The optimum is restricted to choices of J with the other graph data fixed.

## Six zero-sum necklace identities — one counting theorem

Catalog IDs are oeis-a208598, oeis-a208599, oeis-a208600, oeis-a208601 (rotations, 4–7 beads), and oeis-a208826, oeis-a208827 (rotations and reversal, 5–6 beads); e.g. https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a208598 . These are six individually listed targets and two group actions, not six unrelated proof inventions. Sources: https://oeis.org/A208598 , https://oeis.org/A208599 , https://oeis.org/A208600 , https://oeis.org/A208601 , https://oeis.org/A208826 , https://oeis.org/A208827 . The parent array https://oeis.org/A208597 includes Andrew Howroyd's 2017 divisor-sum code before Colin Barker's 2018 proposals on the individual records. The following derives all six exact generating functions. We do not claim the general counting method as original.

Let H_d(n) count d-tuples from the integers [−n,n] summing to zero. By shifting all entries by n and inclusion–exclusion on entries >2n,
H_d(n)=∑_{0≤j≤⌊(d−1)/2⌋} (−1)^j binom(d,j) binom((d−2j)n+d−1−j,d−1).
This works for all n≥0: any term with negative residual sum in the displayed range gives an ordinary binomial with top between 0 and d−2, hence vanishes. The excluded terms have negative residual. H_d is a polynomial of degree at most d−1. For a k-bead necklace counted up to rotations, every rotation by r fixes repeated blocks of length gcd(k,r); the total sum is zero exactly when one block sums to zero. Burnside gives
C_k(n)=(1/k)∑_{d|k} φ(k/d) H_d(n).
Consequently its generating function starting at n=1 has denominator (1−x)^k. An independent route uses the constant term of (∑_{s=−n}^n z^s)^d for the same fixed-block count.

For 5 beads each reflection fixes one coordinate and swaps two pairs. The equation is a+2(b+c)=0. With t=⌊n/2⌋, the count fixed by one reflection is F=(2t+1)(2n+1)−t(t+1). Therefore D_5=(C_5+F)/2. F is (7n²+10n+4)/4 for even n and (7n²+4n+1)/4 for odd n. This yields precisely the two quartic formulas on A208826 by substitution into C_5; parity dependence has degree at most one, so the denominator divides (1−x)^5(1+x)^2. For 6 beads three reflections consist of three swaps, with H_3(n) fixed words each. Three have two fixed vertices and two swaps, with (a+b)+2(c+d)=0. Their common fixed-word count is R=∑_{s=−n}^n(2n+1−|s|)(2n+1−2|s|), a polynomial of degree at most three. Thus D_6=C_6/2+(H_3+R)/4 has degree at most five.

The coefficient vectors below are in ascending powers of x for the numerator after its common leading x is factored out.

These numerators are recoverable by convolving the first deg(denominator) positive-index terms of the exact Burnside count against the displayed denominator. The degree/parity argument proves all remaining coefficients vanish. It therefore also proves the respective linear recurrences, including their stated onset. A canonical-orbit brute-force enumeration at n=1,2 checks the rotation/reversal convention independently.

## Two reflection-axis identities for binary necklaces

Catalog: https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a185376 and https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a185378 . Statements: https://oeis.org/A185376 and https://oeis.org/A185378 . Literature on symmetric necklaces and periods: Petros Hadjicostas, Enumerative Combinatorics and Applications (2023), https://ecajournal.haifa.ac.il/Volume2023/ECA2023_S2A23.pdf . These are two related identities from a second theorem about least periods. An orbit is under rotations only. Let u be the odd part of n, n≥1.

**Exact conclusions.** The number of length-2n binary necklaces admitting a cut that reads as a palindrome is 2^{n−1}+2^{(u−1)/2}. The number invariant under a reflection but admitting no palindromic cut is 2^n−2^{(u−1)/2}.

**Proof.** Index sites by residues mod 2n. All reflections have the form j↦c−j; even c gives an axis through vertices, odd c through edges. A palindromic cut means that some edge-axis fixes the word. For a necklace of least period d, its distinct rotational representatives correspond to shifts k modulo d. If one representative admits an axis c, the shifts fixed by a prescribed axis e solve 2k≡e−c (mod d), up to reversing the sign of k, and the count is unchanged by that sign. All axes fixing the same representative coincide modulo d, since composing two gives a period. For even d a reflective orbit therefore contributes exactly two words to a fixed axis of its own parity and zero to a fixed other-parity axis. For odd d it contributes exactly one to each. The total O of reflective necklaces with odd d divides into the same necklaces as those of length u, by repeating or restricting a period block: d|2n odd iff d|u. In length u each contributes exactly one word fixed by a prescribed reflection, whose (u+1)/2 coordinate-orbits give O=2^{(u+1)/2}. A prescribed edge-axis on 2n sites fixes 2^n words and a prescribed vertex-axis fixes 2^{n+1}. Thus, writing E and V for even-period orbits of the two types, 2E+O=2^n and 2V+O=2^{n+1}. Palindromic cuts are counted by E+O; reflection without any palindromic cut is counted by V. Substitution gives the assertions. This argument includes n=1 and constant words. It extends to q colors by replacing base 2 in the word counts by q while retaining the divisor 2 from rotating representatives. Counting axes as if every necklace were primitive would omit precisely the odd-period correction.

## A182084 — extremal graphs of diameter two and girth four

Live record https://lefttosolve.com/p/oeis-a182084 (API `kind=unformalized`, `solution=null`). Original https://oeis.org/A182084 gives offset 2, first value 5, and proposes the minimum 3n−n/p, with p the smallest prime factor of n. Taken literally this fails at n=2. A finite connected 2-regular graph is a cycle, and the only one with girth four is the bipartite C₄. For every n≥3 the exact claim holds. With girth at least four the formula holds also for n=2, realized by C₅.

**Structural lower bound.** Suppose G is an n-regular graph of order N satisfying the hypotheses. It is triangle-free, and diameter two makes it maximal triangle-free since adding any missing edge closes a common-neighbor triangle. If N≥3n the lower bound is immediate. Otherwise its minimum degree exceeds N/3. By Brandt–Thomassé's classification, G is a proper blow-up of an Andrásfai or Vega graph J. Each such J itself has a regular proper blow-up of degree k and order 3k−1 for an integer k≥2. The Andrásfai graphs themselves have this property. The Vega graphs have the weighting in Fact 4.2 of Łuczak–Polcyn–Reiher. The skeleton K₂ is excluded by nonbipartiteness. Write s_v and w_v for vertex-class sizes in the two blow-ups. Each vertex v satisfies Σ_{u∼v} s_u=n and Σ_{u∼v} w_u=k. The symmetric double sum Σ_{v,u∼v} w_v s_u is therefore both (3k−1)n and kN. Coprimality of k,3k−1 gives k∣n, hence k≥p and N=(3−1/k)n≥(3−1/p)n.

**Sharp construction.** Take the Cayley graph on the cyclic group of order 3p−1 with symmetric step set S=[p,2p−1], the p-th Andrásfai graph. The sumset S+S is [−(p−1),p−1] modulo 3p−1, the entire complement of S. This proves triangle-freeness and diameter two. The cycle 0,p,2p,1,p+1,0 has length five. Blow up each vertex to an independent set of size n/p, joining adjacent classes completely. This yields degree n on (3p−1)n/p vertices and preserves triangle-freeness, diameter two and the 5-cycle. For p≥3, vertices 0 and 1 share at least two neighbors in [p+1,2p−1], giving a 4-cycle. For p=2 and even n≥4, the classes on the two ends of any edge have at least two vertices each, again giving a 4-cycle. Girth is therefore exactly four in the required range.

**References.** S. Brandt and S. Thomassé, Dense triangle-free graphs are four-colorable, classification manuscript https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/stephan.thomasse/liste/vega11.pdf . T. Łuczak, J. Polcyn and Chr. Reiher, Andrásfai and Vega graphs in Ramsey–Turán theory, Theorem 5.1 and Fact 4.2, article https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.22682 and open text https://epub.sub.uni-hamburg.de/epub/volltexte/2020/111861/pdf/840.pdf . The latter gives the exact blow-up form explicitly immediately after Theorem 5.1. The regularity/divisibility argument above is a consequence of this established classification; priority for the corollary has not been established.
