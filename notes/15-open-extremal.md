# Odd covers, Latin squares, Sidon sets, and diameter-critical graphs

## Erdős 7. Distinct odd covering systems — open

https://lefttosolve.com/p/Erdos7.erdos_7 . Can residue classes with distinct odd moduli greater than 1 cover the integers? Balister, Bollobás, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba rule out the squarefree case [Inventiones 2022, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-021-01087-5]. Dai et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15110 (November 2026), further prove that the least common multiple L of a hypothetical system must be divisible by 45, and its smallest modulus must be at most 9. These are necessary conditions.

**Worked avoidance criterion.** Factor L into prime powers, ordered in any way, and let M be the product of factors already processed. Suppose q^e is next. For j=1,…,e let c_j be the number of proposed moduli of the form d q^j with d dividing M. A residue modulo M already avoiding all processed congruences has q^e lifts modulo Mq^e. For each compatible class with modulus d q^j, precisely a fraction 1/q^j of these lifts hits the class, by the Chinese remainder theorem. The union bound therefore preserves at least a fraction 1−Σ_j c_j/q^j of the fiber. If the sum is below one at every stage, a final surviving residue certifies that the proposed system fails, for every assignment of residues. Distinctness gives c_j≤τ(M), the number of divisors of M. In particular τ(M)≤q−1 at every stage suffices, since Σ_{j=1}^e q^{-j}=(1−q^{-e})/(q−1). This permits indefinitely many primes and non-squarefree moduli when the successive primes are sufficiently separated. For example no family with distinct nontrivial moduli dividing L=3²·5²·11·23^e can cover, for any e≥1. The prefix divisor counts are 1,3,9,18. Successive surviving fractions are at least 5/9, 7/25, 2/11, and 2/11, giving total density at least 28/5445. Even for e=1 the sum of reciprocals of all nontrivial divisors is (13/9)(31/25)(12/11)(24/23)−1>1, so the unconditional density test cannot give this conclusion. These orders are divisible by 45.

**Second route.** Even without intermediate conditioning, no odd system supported on at most two distinct primes can cover. Its total class density is at most the sum of reciprocals of all nontrivial divisors of L, strictly below (1−1/3)^{-1}(1−1/5)^{-1}−1=7/8. For the general problem, close small primes and repeated powers can make the fiber sum exceed one. The promising improvement is to account for overlaps or choose previously surviving residues with lower subsequent coverage; a uniform bound strong enough for arbitrary moduli is missing. The cited quantitative distortion methods go further than the elementary certificate.

## Erdős 724. Square-root growth for mutually orthogonal Latin squares — open

https://lefttosolve.com/p/Erdos724.erdos_724 . Write f(n) for the maximum size of a collection of pairwise orthogonal Latin squares of order n. Does one absolute c>0 work in f(n)≥c√n for every sufficiently large n? The Erdős archive lists Beth's 1/14.8 exponent; Boyadzhiyska, Das and Szabó explicitly cite M. G. Lu, The maximum number of mutually orthogonal Latin squares, Kexue Tongbao (English Ed.) 30 (1985), 154–159, for the sharper all-orders lower exponent 1/14.3, and distinguish known fixed-k enumeration from growing-k bounds, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.02753 (p. 2); archive https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/724 .

**Worked construction.** For a prime power q, take squares L_a(x,y)=ax+y on the field of q elements for every nonzero a. Each row and column is a permutation; two specified outputs for a≠b determine x by subtraction and then determine y. Thus f(q)≥q−1. To see equality, permute symbols in each square to make its first row identical. At cell (2,1) all entries must differ from the first-row entry in column 1 and must be pairwise distinct, since a repeated entry there would duplicate a diagonal output pair already present in the first row. Hence f(n)≤n−1 for every order n≥2.

A product of two collections with t orthogonal squares at orders q and r gives t orthogonal squares at order qr: for index i apply the two squares separately to the two row coordinates and the two column coordinates, returning the ordered pair of outputs. Latinness and orthogonality follow coordinatewise. In particular f(qr)≥min(q,r)−1 when q,r are prime powers. For the explicit infinite orders n=2^a3^b, b the least integer with 3^b≥2^a, we get f(n)≥2^a−1>√(n/3)−1. These are all composite and divisible by six. Along a subsequence the constant approaches one: the irrationality of log(2)/log(3) and Dirichlet approximation give positive integers a,b with |a log 2−b log 3|→0 and a→∞. Then min(2^a,3^b)/√(2^a3^b)→1, so this same product construction gives f(n)≥(1−o(1))√n along such orders. More generally, for comparable prime powers q≤r≤Cq, f(qr)≥√(qr/C)−1.

**Second route and bottleneck.** A family of k squares is equivalent to n² rows with k+2 coordinates in which every pair of coordinates contains each ordered symbol pair exactly once. The first two coordinates record row and column. An additional square is a partition into n classes of size n, each of which meets every old coordinate-symbol fiber once. This gives a concrete disjoint-transversal packing target for an iterative construction. Existence estimates for fixed k do not imply such a simultaneous family at k≈√n. For n=2p with p an odd prime the direct-product guarantee is merely one, exemplifying the obstruction. A universal construction would need control for these unbalanced orders as well. The elementary product construction and the all-orders estimates are established methods.

## Erdős 156. Small maximal Sidon sets — full upper bound in a recent preprint

https://lefttosolve.com/p/Erdos156.erdos_156 . Is the smallest size s(N) of an inclusion-maximal Sidon set in [1,N] bounded by O(N^{1/3})? Ruzsa's established upper bound is O((N log N)^{1/3}) [https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1009757824153]. Howe, Huffman, Kohlmeyer and Kruer, Small maximal integer Sidon sets by finite phase colorings, Theorem 1.1, posted November 21, 2026, state the full O(N^{1/3}) result and a linked Lean verification [https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00767v1]. Their seed leaves two intervals of length O(p²) and a single residue class modulo p, for p comparable to N^{1/3}. A Sidon subset of an interval of length B has O(√B) elements by its distinct positive differences. The same applies after rescaling the single residue class, which contains O(N/p)=O(p²) possible points. Hence a maximal completion adds O(p) points. The uniform seed construction is the substantive theorem imported from this preprint; its Lean artifact was not rerun here. Pinned manuscript and verification notes: https://github.com/JENW1N/erdos-156-proof/blob/a9e52bd1d056f26888db473ab7320bd9768c2c6b/paper/README.md .

**Independent matching lower bound with an explicit constant.** Write m=|A| and let e,o count its even and odd elements. Every x outside a maximal Sidon A must be of one of the forms b+c−a or (b+c)/2 with a,b,c∈A; on each side of a nontrivial sum equality, cancel occurrences of x if possible. In the first form x outside A forces a different from both b,c. When b=c there are m(m−1) choices, and when b<c there are at most C(m,2)(m−2). In the midpoint form only distinct same-parity pairs can contribute, at most C(e,2)+C(o,2)=C(m,2)−eo. Adding the m original elements gives N≤(m³+m)/2−eo. Thus s(N)≥(2N)^{1/3}(1−o(1)). Combined with the cited upper theorem this gives the correct order with no logarithm. The optimal asymptotic constant is a separate question.

## Erdős 742. Murty–Simon diameter-two-critical graphs — full preprint resolution

https://lefttosolve.com/p/Erdos742.erdos_742 . The conjecture is true for every order. Sanjana Das and Jacob Fox, A proof of the Murty–Simon conjecture on diameter-2-critical graphs [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39032], Theorem 1.1 (October 28, 2026), give the upper bound floor(n²/4) and characterize equality by the balanced complete bipartite graph. For a maximal matching of size m they prove 2|E|≤C(n,2)+m; inserting m≤floor(n/2) and separating the parity of n gives exactly 2|E|≤2 floor(n²/4). Their equality argument forces triangle-freeness, so Mantel's equality case finishes. Independently, the balanced complete bipartite graph has both the stated size and criticality: deleting any edge separates its endpoints by distance at least three (or disconnects the graph). Earlier approaches via stability and regularity proved the theorem only for sufficiently large n. The matching inequality is invoked from the paper; the full result is attributable to Das and Fox.
