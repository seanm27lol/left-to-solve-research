## MO 380970 — Maximizing binary linear independence
Catalog https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-380970 . Original https://mathoverflow.net/q/380970 . The original comments cite general asymptotic singularity results of Kahn–Komlós and Vinh, and ask about smoothing two weights. Source theorem: Anari, Oveis Gharan & Vinzant, “Log-Concave Polynomials I” (2018), https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00929 (also https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2018-Papers/pdfs/59f035.pdf). Direct application plus an equality argument.

For 2≤m≤n, uniform measure on the nonzero vectors of F₂ⁿ is the **unique** maximizing law. The probability is ∏_{j=0}^{m−1}(2ⁿ−2ʲ)/(2ⁿ−1), with the denominator in each factor. For m=1 every law giving 0 mass to the zero vector wins; for m>n all laws give success probability zero.

Proof. Write E=F₂ⁿ∖{0}. A law with positive probability of success and 0<p(0)<1 improves strictly on conditioning to E, since success requires every vector chosen in E; a law with zero success cannot maximize because uniform has positive success. Let B(x)=Σ_I∏_{v∈I}x_v where I ranges over independent m-subsets of E. Success probability is m!B(p). These I are the bases of the rank-m truncation of the vector matroid, so log B is concave on the positive orthant by the cited theorem. Average p over the finite group GL(n,2). Every transform has the same B-value, and the average is uniform u because the action is transitive on E. Jensen gives B(u)≥B(p); extend to the closed simplex by continuity applying it to (1−t)p+t u. If p were a second maximizer, log-concavity would make B constant on the segment between it and u. The diagonal of D²B(u) is zero because B is multiaffine. Every off-diagonal entry is the same positive constant c since two distinct nonzero binary vectors are independent, all ordered pairs form one orbit and every pair extends to an independent m-set. For tangent v=p−u, vᵀD²B(u)v=−c‖v‖². Thus the segment cannot be constant. The value of the optimum follows by exposing vectors in sequence; after j independent draws the span contains 2ʲ−1 forbidden nonzero vectors. Checks: 462 integer-weighted laws of denominator 5 on the seven nonzero vectors for (n,m)=(3,3), using direct triples.

## MO 297859 — Partitioning coupon draws
Catalog https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-297859 . Original https://mathoverflow.net/q/297859 . The prior comments request an example and supply no formula. Related general framework: H. J. Ryser, “Combinatorial Properties of Matrices of Zeros and Ones” (1957), https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1957-044-3 . The cyclic construction below dispenses with a graph realization theorem.

Let a_i be the counts in m independent uniform draws from n types, and k≥1 the group size cap. Fix a realization a. The minimum group count and the maximum number of full groups, respectively, are
B(a)=max(max_i a_i, ceil(m/k)),
R(a)=max{r=0,…,floor(m/k): Σ_i min(a_i,r)≥kr}.
For either f=B or f=R its requested expectation is the *explicit finite sum*
E f = m!/n^m Σ_{a_1+…+a_n=m} f(a)/(a_1!⋯a_n!).
For m=0 both are zero. If k>n, R=0 when k>1; the formula already handles this.

Necessity: a group contains at most one of any type and at most k coupons. r full groups require exactly kr slots and use at most min(a_i,r) of type i. For B sufficiency, list the copies of each type consecutively and deal the list cyclically to B boxes. Each type-run has length ≤B, so no box gets two. All loads are floor(m/B) or ceil(m/B), hence at most k. For R sufficiency at r≥1 choose integers 0≤b_i≤min(a_i,r) totalling kr; this is possible whenever summed capacities suffice. Deal these copies consecutively by type round-robin to r boxes. Each box gets exactly k distinct coupons. Leftovers can be singletons. For k=1 the maximal r is m, leaving no leftovers. The large example yields (6,4). Independent exhaustive recursion over first legal boxes verified 3,570 count/cap cases through 4 types, 8 coupons and k≤5.

## MO 409374 — A sharper coin-race inequality
Catalog https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-409374 . Original https://mathoverflow.net/q/409374 . Crosspost https://math.stackexchange.com/q/4314944 . Neither page had an answer in the API. One MO comment suggests coupling the two coins; another correctly notes that this would change the question's joint law. Related method: Condorcet jury theorems, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jury-theorems/ .

Let A=Pr(B_k(p)>B_k(q))−Pr(B_k(p)<B_k(q)) for independent binomials, p>q, k≥1. Put δ=p−q and d=p(1−q)+q(1−p)>0. Uniform exact bound:
A≥(δ/d)[1−(1−d)^k]≥δ>0.
Proof. Pair the independent Bernoulli trials of the two players, retaining independence both across pairs and within a pair. The number L of discordant pairs is Bin(k,d). Conditional on L=ℓ≥1 their signs are iid, with positive probability θ=p(1−q)/d≥1/2. Let S be their sum. For each integer r>0, Pr(S=r)≥Pr(S=−r); for θ<1 this follows from the likelihood ratio, and θ=1 is immediate. Pairing these masses gives E sgn(S)−E(S/ℓ)=Σ_{r>0}(1−r/ℓ)[Pr(S=r)−Pr(S=−r)]≥0. Since E(S/ℓ)=2θ−1=δ/d, averaging over L proves both inequalities. Verified via exact rational binomial sums for 288 cases through k=8.

Sharper asymptotic in the question's regime: if q→1/2 and kδ→0, then A~2δ√(k/π). More generally if q stays bounded away from 0 and 1, A~δ√[k/(πq(1−q))]. Write b_{j,t}=Pr(B_{k−1}(t)=j) and let Y∼Bin(k,q). Differentiating E sgn(B_k(t)−Y) in t gives
k Σ_j b_{j,t}[Pr(Y=j)+Pr(Y=j+1)]
(by differentiating the binomial polynomial, the derivative of E f(B_k(t)) is k E[f(B_{k−1}(t)+1)−f(B_{k−1}(t))]). At t=q, the sum equals Pr(D=0)+Pr(D=1) for D a difference of two independent Bin(k−1,q)'s, by writing B_k(q) as B_{k−1}(q) plus one Bernoulli. Both point masses are asymptotic to 1/√(4π(k−1)q(1−q)). Indeed Fourier inversion uses [1−4q(1−q)sin²(s/2)]^{k−1} e^{−irs}; scaling s by 1/√((k−1)q(1−q)) gives e^{−u²}, dominated by e^{−4u²/π²} using sin(|s|/2)≥|s|/π on |s|≤π. The ratio b_{j,t}/b_{j,q} is exp(O(kδ)) uniformly for t∈[q,p] and all j, since q stays inside (0,1) and the log derivative has magnitude O(k). The whole integrand therefore stays within 1+o(1) of its diagonal value. Integrate from q to p with A(q,q)=0.

## MO 435603 — Sharp bound for second moment of a return on a finite tree
Catalog https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-435603 . Original https://mathoverflow.net/q/435603 . Nicolas Rivera's original comment explicitly points to the stationary hitting-time identity, Aldous & Fill, Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs, eq. (2.22), https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.html . We derive a tree-size formula and a sharp fixed-degree bound from it.

For a tree with m≥1 edges rooted at v, d=deg(v), let s_e be the number of vertices below oriented edge e, and τ the first strictly positive return to v of the non-lazy simple random walk. Then
E_v τ²=(2m+2Σ_e(2s_e−1)²)/d.
Write F(s)=s(4s²−1)/3 for s≥0. The **sharp fixed-(m,d) bound** is [2m+2F(m−d+1)+2(d−1)]/d. It is attained by a path of length m−d+1 from v and d−1 additional leaf neighbors. In particular over all starting points the bound (8m³+4m)/3 is sharp, attained at an endpoint of a path. The isolated 1-vertex tree admits no uniform choice of neighbor under the stated walk; under an absorbing convention τ=1.

Proof. For a child x, the expected time h_x to hit its parent satisfies h_x=deg(x)+Σ_{y child of x}h_y by first-step analysis, hence h_x=2s_e−1 by induction. For any vertex the hitting time mean to v sums these along its ancestral path. Under stationary law π(x)=deg(x)/(2m), the far-side mass of e is (2s_e−1)/(2m), so E_π T_v=Σ_e(2s_e−1)²/(2m), taking T_v=0 when already at v. Kac gives E_v τ=2m/d. In a return interval of length τ, the sum of the forward waiting times at starting times 0,…,τ−1 is τ(τ−1)/2, including the visit at time zero with wait zero. By regeneration this gives E_v τ²=E_v τ(1+2E_π T_v), valid also for periodic walks. This proves the identity. At each of the d branches of size b, list its b vertices in postorder so a vertex at position j satisfies s_e≤j. Thus the contribution is at most Σ_{j=1}^b(2j−1)²=F(b), attained by a path. The d branch sizes are positive with sum m, and F has strictly increasing differences, so their sum of F's is maximal at (m−d+1,1,…,1). A star at its center has τ=2 and the formula gives four. Direct exact solution of the first- and second-hitting-time linear systems agreed for all 700 rooted labelled trees on two through five vertices.

## MO 88382 — Capped coupon collector, any partial target
Catalog https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-88382 . Original https://mathoverflow.net/q/88382 . Richard Stanley’s original comment points to Russell May, “Coupon Collecting with Quotas,” EJC 15, N31 (2008), https://doi.org/10.37236/906 . May treats completion of all quotas. Our direct dynamic programming formula covers partially filled unequal quotas.

For type probabilities a_i≥0 summing to one, integer caps d_i≥0 and N target valid coupons, let F(x) be expected remaining draws given already retained counts x_i≤d_i. If Σx_i≥N, F(x)=0. If N>Σ_{i:a_i>0}d_i the expected time starting at zero is infinite, because the target is unreachable. Otherwise on reachable transient states define A(x)=Σ_{i:x_i<d_i}a_i>0. First-step conditioning gives
F(x)=[1+Σ_{i:x_i<d_i, a_i>0}a_i F(x+e_i)]/A(x).
This finite backward recurrence ends at total N and F(0) answers the full original question. Finiteness follows because whenever a further valid coupon exists, its trial success chance is at least min_{a_i>0}a_i>0, and at most N successful steps are needed. Alternative exact route: arrivals in a unit-rate Poisson process split independently into rates a_i. Use a_i=0 separately as above. For x≥0 let
G_i(z,t)=Σ_{r=0}^{d_i−1}e^{−a_i t}(a_it)^r z^r/r! + z^{d_i}(1−Σ_{r=0}^{d_i−1}e^{−a_it}(a_it)^r/r!).
Then E M=∫₀^∞ Σ_{j=0}^{N−1} [z^j]∏G_i(z,t)dt. The integrand is Pr(the threshold has not been hit at t); the expected elapsed interarrival time equals E M by independence of the rate-one arrival gaps and their coupon labels. This includes classical coupon collecting when all caps are 1 and N=L.

## Two mathematical controls already answered in source comments
MO 279794, https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-279794 , https://mathoverflow.net/q/279794 . The ballot theorem was explicitly identified, with formula N/T for a positive endpoint, by Christian Remling in the original comments. For T≥1, |N|≤T with matching parity and positive conditioning probability, the exact result is |N|/T; N=0 gives zero. For N>0 write u=(T+N)/2. A positive path starts with +1. Reflect the remainder of a path that hits 0 before ending at N; subtracting bad paths yields C(T−1,u−1)−C(T−1,u)=(N/T) C(T,u). Negative endpoints follow by sign reversal. For independent biased steps each conditioned path still has the same probability. Exhaustively checked through T=12.

MO 333962, https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-333962 , https://mathoverflow.net/q/333962 . The original comment by fedja already gives inclusion–exclusion. For independently retained A⊆[n] with probabilities p_A, the exact cover probability is Σ_{B⊆[n]}(−1)^{|B|}∏_{A:A∩B≠∅}(1−p_A). Event that no point of B is covered requires exactly those intersecting subsets to be absent. An independent derivation is to write the indicator of full coverage as ∏_i(1−1_{i is missed}), expand, and average. The empty chosen subset does not affect coverage, and the empty-universe case gives one.
