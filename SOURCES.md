# Sources and attribution

The [upstream catalog snapshot](https://github.com/GuillaumeBAUD-coder/open-math-agents/tree/db6b545e55c87123a38800707fa0f499fc8e6157/data) supplies the problem IDs, titles, and catalog labels. The [problem index](INDEX.md) links each entry to its catalog record, and the research notes link the underlying questions, papers, and source comments.

* OEIS entries credit the contributors listed on the corresponding sequence pages. The OEIS Foundation makes its database available under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://oeis.org/wiki/The_OEIS_End-User_License_Agreement).
* MathOverflow and Mathematics Stack Exchange posts credit the original authors and contributors on the linked threads. Their licenses vary with the date of contribution. See [Stack Exchange content licensing](https://stackoverflow.com/help/licensing).
* Wikipedia-sourced questions link to their upstream records. Google DeepMind's [Formal Conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures) provides Lean targets for entries in the main catalog. Each research note identifies any specific prior work it uses.
* Earlier proofs remain credited to their authors. In particular, the original three-digit observation in A056142 is recorded by David Wasserman. The restricted four-square theorem follows from the classification by Chae, Ji, Kim, Kim, Oh, and Yoon.

The mathematical arguments, searches, code, and manuscript preparation in this collection were AI-assisted. Individual notes record the role of prior theorems and computations.
